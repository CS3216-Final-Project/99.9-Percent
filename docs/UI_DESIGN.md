# UI design for 99.99%

During Chapter 0, enable only the game controls relevant to the current lesson. Dim the whole game except the current lesson's control, mark unrelated controls unavailable and remove them from keyboard navigation; pointer interactions and game shortcuts must obey the same boundary. Keep the guide's Back/Next, sound, and Menu available at every lesson. Exit in Menu saves before leaving, with no duplicate button in the guide's header. All Menu tabs and their controls remain usable; the guide steps aside while Menu or another real dialog is open, and closing it returns to the same lesson. During a completed player's replay, show a visible Return to game button in the guide and above the Menu tabs. It exits practice and restores the paused original company, including after reload or Save and exit. First-time players still finish Chapter 0 before continuing. Purchase quotes cannot be confirmed during Chapter 0: the quote lesson opens a real quote and the next lesson enables its real Cancel button. Escape and room clicks must not dismiss it. Resuming the Cancel lesson restores a real available quote on the correct floor. Remove all temporary restrictions when the guide ends or unmounts.

Once Chapter 0 has been completed, its replay is optional. Show Return to game in the replay guide and Menu, and Exit walkthrough in the chapter overview, including when an incident or review hides the guide. The overview labels an active completed-profile walkthrough as In progress and leaves Opening available. Exiting clears only the replay, returns to the existing chapter paused and preserves the company and prior completion. First-time players still finish Chapter 0 explicitly.

This guide records the current playable game's design. It applies to both modes - presented to players as Guided Mode and Challenge Mode - including later phases. Gameplay rules may change; the shared visual language and interaction patterns should remain familiar. The proposal's future 2D direction is planning context and does not replace the current room or UI without a separate design decision.

## Approved Warm Classic patterns

The whole playable game uses Warm Classic: title and account screens, both modes, Chapter 0, later chapters, room controls, incidents, purchases, reports, Tech, Team, History, settings and feedback. Use cream paper surfaces, dark plum text and primary actions, warm gold accents, blue information, green success, lavender selection and restrained red/orange status throughout.

- **Flat controls:** 14 px rounded rectangles, thin warm borders, no bevels, hard offset shadows, press movement or inset shading. Only floating layers have soft diffuse shadows. Hover/press change fill and border; blue focus differs from lavender selection. Keep touch targets at least 44 px wherever practical and reserve stable button dimensions across states.
- **Icons:** shared Lucide icons in pastel concept tiles. Tech is gold and History blue, with matching tile geometry. Run/Pause has a plain icon without a yellow tile. Icon-only controls have accessible names; status uses text as well as colour.
- **Menu:** At a glance has a dashboard icon and separate Week, Game second, Users and Cash icon cards. Autosaved/Not saved is a quiet summary status. Matching compact Resume, Save now, How to play and Exit buttons share one row; Resume is primary and Exit uses logout. A failed save replaces the menu with a confirmation offering Cancel, Save and exit, or explicit Exit without saving. Preserve unreadable existing saves.
- **Clock:** calendar Week N, clock current second / 60 s and slim weekly progress. This is an operating week, not a maximum game duration. No separate Total seconds line. Preserve the authoritative elapsed counter for saves, tutorial logic and test instrumentation.
- **Workspace:** open by default; Hide equipment and actions collapses it to Equipment & actions. Reopening restores focus. Selecting equipment or entering an incident reveals it. It stays available during the mandatory guide and purchase quotes. No redundant System overview heading or divider.
- **Inspection:** timer, error and backlog symptom icons above Inspect equipment, without numbered steps. The Inspect equipment heading is plain text, without a tooltip or dotted underline. Selected equipment has its matching icon, name, Live tag and exact readings inline. Clicking the selected equipment chip again dismisses its readings; selecting another replaces them. Room inspections still select and reveal the equipment. No separate Full system evidence section or repeated measurement timestamp and utilisation explainer. Use the latest completed snapshot and preserve units; distinguish installed/routed capacity, measured/configured cache values, and actual/detected health.
- **Incident recovery:** keep chapter requirements above the Latency, Errors and Backlog cards during both healthy play and incidents. Below those readings, show Recovery progress with its game-second counter and always-visible conditions: latency below 500 ms, service errors below 1%, and accepted traffic with completed requests, for five consecutive game seconds. Explain that an unhealthy second resets progress and pausing freezes it. For an active failed server, also show that it must be restored or isolated from traffic with an empty queue. Recovery rules stay in the simulation.
- **Actions:** full name first; bold wallet cost and clock activation duration beneath it, left aligned. Purchases and incident responses share the structure while retaining their own engine rules and units. Keep unavailable reasons reachable and destructive changes reversible until confirmed.

Review all screens and every equipment selection at desktop and narrow mobile sizes, including keyboard focus, collapsed/open panels, loading/empty/error/success/disabled states, tooltips, long values and reduced motion. Preserve the scene, simulation, save compatibility and Chapter 0 restrictions above.

## Visual language and reusable components

Use the shared font-size constants in `index.css`: `--text-xs` (0.8 rem), `--text-sm` (0.9 rem), `--text-md` (1 rem), `--text-lg` (1.15 rem), `--text-xl` (1.35 rem), `--text-2xl` (1.6 rem), `--text-3xl` (2.1 rem) and `--text-4xl` (2.9 rem). Body text starts at md; use sm and xs for secondary labels and larger sizes for headings and prominent values. Preserve browser zoom. Responsive display titles can interpolate between shared endpoints; ordinary text and controls use the scale rather than one-off sizes.

The isometric room is the main canvas. Warm Classic uses a dark plum HUD, cream paper panels, smooth Lucide icons and plum primary actions with pastel concept tiles. The agreed direction is warm and playful with flat controls: cream, plum and gold, colourful concept icons, a shared 14 px button radius, a 22 px panel radius and thin warm borders. Buttons, tabs, icon tiles, sliders and inputs never use bevels, inset shading, hard offset shadows or press-down movement. Soft diffuse shadows belong only to floating layers. Hover and press change colour and border without moving a control. The bottom dock uses the same dark purple shell as the top HUD in both modes. Its group labels and clock use light text; navigation controls stay cream, icon tiles stay pastel and selected view tabs stay lavender with plum labels. Tech uses a warm gold icon tile against its lavender selection so the icon never blends into the selected surface. The top HUD groups its four metrics on one cream surface with quiet internal dividers, pastel concept tiles, sentence-case labels and prominent plum values. Cash and App Health retain their actions, hover feedback and visible keyboard focus. Warning and failure values use the dark status inks against paper. Header Cash, Users and App Health keep their icons visible, including on phones. Use the tokens in `frontend/src/index.css` for colours, fonts, borders, radii, spacing and shadows. Space Grotesk is the face for the logo, headings, controls, labels, metrics and room lettering; Source Sans 3 is the body face for sentences, longer explanations, tooltips and text entry. These are the only two game typefaces, bundled locally. Numbers use tabular figures. Keep canvas font loading and redraws in sync with the interface fonts so room signs, speech bubbles and billboards use the same typography. Do not introduce a separate theme or a compressed text-only HUD for a new mode.

Buttons must keep the same width and height across their states at a given viewport size. Account for every label, icon, badge and loading indicator before choosing dimensions: clicking, toggling, disabling, loading data or completing a request must not resize a control or move neighbouring controls. Reserve sufficient space from the first render with a shared size or a layout sized for the largest supported state; do not shrink the text to make a later state fit. Responsive sizes may change with the viewport, but state changes must not change the layout. For example, the home-screen Account chip must reserve its final footprint while account data loads and keep it when switching between Sign in and the player's identity; constrain long names within that space while keeping the full name accessible. Apply the same rule to Run/Pause, changing speed labels, counts and pending/success/error button labels.

The room itself is a realistic office, apart from the HUD's purple: warm off-white walls, concrete, grey carpet tiles where the crew work and coloured carpets in the rooms, charcoal and grey metal racks, white and light grey furniture, with a few colour accents. Its colours live in `frontend/src/components/scene/palette.ts` (`ROOM_PALETTE`, `FURNITURE`, `EQUIPMENT`, `PIXEL_FLOORS`); take new room colours from there rather than writing hex values into the scene. The HUD tokens in `index.css` are for the interface only.

The building has two floors on one footprint (`game/floors.ts`, `scene/levels.ts`): the office on top and the server floor below, where every machine stands; the people's desks (Deploy, Team, Growth) stay upstairs. The room shows one floor at a time, and only the floor the elevator stands at has labels and clickable equipment, so nothing on screen points at a floor that is not drawn. The floor menu opens from Floor (with a layers icon and selected floor number) in Navigation in the shared bottom dock, or from Navigation on phones, like an elevator's panel: one button per floor with its storey number, the floor in view pressed with the shared lavender selection, and a badge on a floor out of view that needs the player (amber for a warning, red and pulsing for an incident, with the reason in the button's accessible name). Switching rides the elevator in 5 seconds: the moving room stays visible through animated shaft shadows and passing landing lights, skipped by a second press, any click or a key, and cut straight through with reduced motion. Choosing equipment from a panel, the inspector or a tutorial step takes the view to its floor; an incident never moves it, but its panel offers Go to the server floor. Page Up and Page Down switch floors except while typing or in a dialog or menu. The floor in view is remembered in this browser, per mode, like the walk mode and camera below it; it is never part of a save.

Walk mode is optional and never the only way to do anything. View mode sits beside Floor and Camera under Navigation. It offers explicit Overview (pan, zoom and rotate), Walk mode (explore on foot), Follow (walk with the camera behind you) and First person (walk seeing through its eyes) choices, with the active choice pressed; V goes from one view to the next. Follow and First person turn walk mode on, and ending the walk returns to the overview centred on where the avatar was, facing the way the view did. On phones the same choices are inside Navigation. Selecting either closes its controls menu and hands the keys back to the room. The avatar wears a yellow ring so it stands out from the crew, and a yellow target marks a click-planned destination until it arrives. What it can reach is a button low in the middle of the room ("Space · Inspect Database"), so a tap works on a phone, with the same words in a polite live region for screen readers. Keys belong first to a text field, a dialog or a menu; the arrows also to a focused button, tab, radio or slider, while Space uses what the avatar can reach even then — only with nothing in reach does a focused control keep it. A fun thing the avatar plays with says so in the same button while its animation runs ("Space · Resting in a nap pod…"), and the avatar moves at a brisk walk with Shift to run. Esc closes in a fixed order: a dialog or controls menu, a view sheet, walk mode, then the selection. Avatars are chosen from pictures in a radio grid, from a chip beside the account's on the home screen or the menu's Avatar tab; the choice is a browser setting, never part of a save.

The views from the avatar use a perspective camera beside the overview's orthographic one (`scene/views.ts`, `scene/ViewRig.tsx`). Moving between the overview and either of them dims to dark, cuts and comes back in 0.6 seconds, with a soft whoosh; between Follow and First person the camera eases. Reduced motion cuts at once. Follow stays inside the room, under the walls' tops and in front of the elevator shaft, so every wall stands. First person draws a ceiling of tiles, light panels and vents, a dot at the centre of the view, and a short hint at the top on how to look round, without keys on a touch screen. Equipment labels shrink with distance seen from the avatar and hide behind it. The city outside is not drawn there: the walls and ceiling hide it, and the windows show it as pictures. The field of view, 70° to 90°, is a slider in the menu's Graphics section.

The map is a paper card in the room's bottom left, clear of the equipment panel on the right and the walk prompt in the middle, and under the equipment sheet on a phone until the sheet is hidden. It is drawn as SVG from `scene/floorplan.ts`, so it cannot disagree with the room. It shows the floor in view with its zones named, glass, the shaft and furniture, the avatar as a yellow arrow, the crew as dots and a red badge where an incident shows symptoms (blue while being investigated). A line underneath says where the avatar is, such as "Lounge, office floor", and is the map's polite live region; the drawing is hidden from screen readers. Tabs change floor like the floor menu, and in walk mode a click on the plan walks the avatar there. A zone glows for a moment as the avatar walks in. The close button folds it to a Map button, remembered in this browser; on a phone it starts folded.

Each zone of the office has a floor of its own and its name floating over it (`ZONE_NAMES` in `scene/floorplan.ts`, `SERVER_ZONE_NAMES` downstairs): a small dark sign drawn in the 3D scene, always facing the camera and 20 px tall at any zoom, smaller than the equipment's labels and fading out when the camera pulls back too far to read it. Zone names are scenery, not interface: they carry no actions, ignore clicks and stay out of the accessibility tree. Machine rooms keep their equipment labels and get no zone name.

## Shared flat control system

Apply this system to both modes and every surface: title and account, HUD and chapters, overview and incidents, operations and work, footer and navigation, all seven menu tabs, purchase confirmations, inspection and reports, tutorials, Tech, Team, History, feedback and forms. The menu is not a separate theme. Night sheets retain their dark workspace; the controls and paper evidence cards use the same geometry and states. Tech, Team and History leave a 16 px outer gap inside the desktop room area and an 8 px gap around the mobile sheet. Clip the contents to the rounded panel, keep the title and Close action fixed above its scrolling body, and inset introductory text from the edges.

| State / role | Shared treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Primary decision | Plum fill, cream label and icon; darker fill while pressed. Use for Resume, Run, start and confirmation actions. |
| Secondary action | Cream fill and a thin warm edge; warmer fill on hover and press. |
| Selected / toggled | Lavender fill and plum edge, plus the existing selected, pressed or checked semantics. Radio choices retain a filled marker; status and selection never depend on colour alone. |
| Destructive action | Red treatment with an explicit action label and the existing confirmation. |
| Keyboard focus | Visible blue 3 px outline with an offset; do not replace it with a shadow or remove it on dark surfaces. |
| Disabled / unavailable | Readable muted text, no movement, and the existing explanation of the requirement or cost. |
| Loading / result | Reserve the control footprint across every label, count and icon change. |

Use `--control-edge`, `--control-inset`, `--control-hover`, `--control-active`, `--selection`, `--selection-edge`, `--primary-hover` and `--primary-active` from `index.css`. `--press` is `none`; `--lift` is a diffuse overlay shadow. Do not add a local palette to solve an individual component. Keep layout transforms, room animations, focus indicators and equipment-targeting rings independent from button decoration.

### Usability review

Use [Jakob Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) as review questions, not a usability score. A visual refresh must preserve these behaviours:

| Heuristic | Game review check |
| --- | --- |
| Visibility of system status | Time, saving, pending work and incident progress remain visible and accurately labelled. |
| Match with the real world | Player-facing equipment names and explicit cost/time units replace internal identifiers. |
| User control and freedom | Cancel, Escape, Resume and restored focus provide clear ways back. |
| Consistency and standards | The same action, state and icon have the same treatment across every game surface. |
| Error prevention | Purchase and unsaved-exit confirmations remain intact; unavailable choices explain why. |
| Recognition rather than recall | Actions keep their labels, costs, delays and contextual evidence. |
| Flexibility and efficiency | Existing shortcuts and full touch controls remain available. |
| Aesthetic and minimalist design | Show the next decision first; put supporting detail in existing disclosures. |
| Error recovery | Preserve plain errors, retry, the entered text and raw-save export. |
| Help and documentation | Keep contextual tooltips, How to play and explanations beside unfamiliar controls. |

## Screen structure

**Walkthrough (Chapter 0)** integrates Guided Mode's How to play walkthrough into the chapter sequence before the six gameplay chapters (which keep their numbers 1–6). Use Walkthrough as the introduction's name in the guide, current chapter, overview, home screen and related controls. View chapters includes the introduction as entry 0 and Replay walkthrough, which preserves the company and its investments; Menu → How to play replays the same introduction. It has ten short lessons, in order: Your job (the goal, with a restaurant comparison: customers, App Server as waiters, Database as the kitchen, the queue as backlog), Look at the App Server, Compare with the Database, Spot trouble (latency, errors, backlog), Run one second, Preview a purchase, Keep your cash (Cancel), Cash and the weekly bill, Speed and floors, and Ready for Chapter 1 (the inspect–decide–run loop and autosave). Each lesson leads with one instruction naming the real control, a reason of at most two sentences, an optional small labelled example or live reading, and a collapsed Learn more for the longer explanation and keyboard shortcuts. Teach only what the Opening needs. Tech, History, saving, routing, caching, incidents and later chapters are introduced when the player reaches them: the History sheet and the Tech tree open with a one-line introduction, the first settled operating week shows a one-card tip on Cash once per profile (never in a practice company), and each later chapter's guide introduces its controls. Chapter 0 is mandatory for new profiles: hold management time until Finish walkthrough is explicitly pressed, apart from the bounded one-second Run exercise. There is no Skip. Save and exit preserves the unfinished lesson. Lessons are identified by id; saved progress stores a versioned index, and progress saved by the earlier 21-lesson walkthrough resumes at the lesson that now teaches the same thing. Preserve already completed profiles.

- **Home screen:** one screen for every visit, whichever mode was played last: a first visit, a Guided Mode run in progress and a Challenge Mode run in progress look the same. The room remains behind the overlay. Account and avatar controls sit above the game name and tag line, without a title kicker, then the Guided Mode and Challenge Mode cards under “Choose how you want to play”, Guided first, stacked on narrow screens. Each card has its pace line, a short explanation (learning infrastructure concepts through six guided chapters versus independently reaching 50,000 users in the week-by-week 26-week challenge), a saved-game box and its own primary button. The saved-game box reads “Chapter n of 6: name” for Guided Mode and “Week n of 26” for Challenge Mode, or “No saved game”; phones hide the empty box so the start buttons show first. The button is Start Guided Mode / Continue company and Start Challenge Mode / Continue week n; Challenge Mode in progress adds New game. A completed Guided Mode walkthrough offers Replay walkthrough; Challenge Mode offers Replay the tutorial once its guide is done: Challenge Mode's restarts the first week, Guided Mode's starts a fresh practice company and restores the original company on Finish or Exit, and a closed Guided Mode company has none. Challenge Mode is locked until the first five Guided Mode chapters are complete: while locked its card shows the lock with “Complete the first five Guided Mode chapters to unlock.” and a disabled “Complete Guided Mode to unlock” button instead of a start control. The mode played last gets a yellow “Last played” tag and keyboard focus on its button. Choosing the other mode switches to it at once and keeps the first mode's save; when the current run cannot be saved, the button turns red as “Switch and lose this run” with a warning, as in the menu. No run starts before a choice. Anonymous analytics is on by default. Until the player answers, an analytics notice (“Help improve 99.99%”) sits below the mode selection, is kept compact (text on the left, buttons on the right, stacked on phones), shows “99.99%” in the logo face, explains what is tracked, that nothing is sent while the browser's Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control setting is on, and offers a primary “OK” and a red “Opt out”; either choice is remembered and the panel does not return. Give feedback sits below.
- **Account:** the chip reads Sign in (with “Save to the cloud”) for guests and shows the player's initials and name once signed in. It opens one Account dialog. Signed out, the dialog explains what an account is for, then shows Continue with Google, an “or use your email” divider and an email form that switches between Sign in and Create an account (optional name, password at least 8 characters with a show/hide toggle). Errors appear under the fields and keep what was typed. When accounts are offline, a warning callout with Try again replaces the status line and every option stays usable. Signed in, the dialog shows the profile with Sign out, then “This browser” (the Guided Mode save and Save to cloud, or why there is nothing to save), “In your account” (cloud saves with their time and version, each with Load, plus Refresh) and a Backup disclosure. Loading a cloud save closes the dialog and returns to the home screen with that save ready to continue. Say “cloud save” and “local save”, never “cloud company”.
- **Top bar:** the brand at the left, four familiar metric tiles, then the sound mute and Menu. The week or incident clock is not here: it sits in the bottom bar beside the buttons that advance it, so the player does not look across the screen between reading the time and moving it on. Health changes to the incident icon and the whole header gets the red striped treatment during an incident. Guided Mode pending revenue must be clearly distinguished from available cash and settled revenue.
- **Guided Mode chapters:** a compact bar below the company stats shows the current chapter number and name, one state-derived goal using `--text-lg` on desktop and phones (17.25 px at the game's default 15 px root size), Next when offered by the existing progression gates, and the existing View chapters button at the right. On phones the goal wraps and Next takes its own full-width row. The Walkthrough introduction and six gameplay chapters live in the paused View chapters overview. Closing restores focus and leaves time paused. A disabled Next has a visible, keyboard-reachable explanation. Keep detailed requirements, pending actions, stability, reviews and warnings in the equipment workspace; do not repeat its generic objective there.
- **Management:** room equipment is clickable. Selecting it gives immediate visible feedback with its name, icon and authoritative metrics. Guided Mode can keep a compact system overview visible because only a few opening actions exist. The chapter bar carries the objective; the overview retains specific next actions and requirements. Response rows show the name, then cost and delay badges underneath, so the narrow panel never scrolls horizontally. The panel scrolls vertically when richer readings or later-stage controls need more room; the dock remains reachable.
- **Incident:** retain the red banner and symptom-focused title, a compact progress area, three impact tiles, then **Inspect equipment** and **Actions**, without number badges. Equipment controls mirror the room. Selected equipment is highlighted and its latest observation is shown. Keep the permitted responses equally prominent; no full metrics table before the response list.
- **Bottom bar:** one dock in both modes. Labeled Tech, Team and History tabs sit on the left (Team is Challenge-only). Navigation groups Floor, View mode and Camera as matching dropdowns. View mode offers Overview, Walk mode, Follow and First person; camera adjustments and keyboard shortcuts remain disclosures. In a view from the avatar the camera's zoom brings Follow closer or farther, and its rotate buttons swing the view. Time groups the clock, selected speed and fixed-size primary action on the right. Guided Mode uses Run/Pause and the current second / 60 s with a weekly progress bar, with secondary **+1s** and **+5s** step buttons (and N for one game second) between the speed and Run, in management and incidents alike. A step pauses a running clock, advances exactly that many game seconds, stops early wherever running would, and is disabled whenever the clock is held. Run/Pause stays the only primary time control. Challenge uses Next week, optional auto-advance in Time controls, and Pause/Resume during incidents. At narrow widths the clock, speed and primary action occupy the top row; labeled view tabs and Navigation occupy the second. Menus open above the entire dock so the clock and primary action remain reachable. Escape closes a menu and returns focus to its trigger; clicking outside closes it. Reserve control dimensions across states and counts.
- **Review:** report in a shared modal with outcome tag, time range, spending and evidence-based explanations. Raw snapshots belong in a disclosure. Continue returns to the same company.
- **Views:** Tech, Team and History open in the same night sheet over the room in both modes, titled "Tech tree", "Team" and "History". The sheet covers the room and side panel but leaves the top and bottom bars; the active tab stays pressed. Closing it, or Esc, returns to the room.
- **History:** the same three tabs in both modes: **Charts**, **Events** and **Incidents (n)**. Use shared chart cards with physical steps or weeks labelled accurately; Guided Mode charts latency and traffic by step and revenue and costs by operating week. Events is the shared feed, newest first, labelled with the mode's time unit. Incidents lists each postmortem as a row that expands to the report; Guided Mode's stage outcomes sit above them on paper cards. Financial settlements and event details can use disclosures.
- **Menu:** use the same cream paper, plum primary action, warm gold accents and colourful concept icons as the dock and panels. Resume is the primary action in the row of flat buttons. Keep visible keyboard focus and selected fills; avoid heavy outlines and raised shadows within this dialog. The same seven tabs, in the same order, in both modes: **Run, Sound, Graphics, Avatar, Saves, Account, Playtest**. From 760 px the tabs form a stable left rail beside the panel. Above the tabs stays what must be seen whichever tab is open: the run card and any unreadable-save warning. Resume sits first in the Run tab's row of matching, compact icon-and-label buttons; explanations use tooltips rather than card descriptions. The card is "At a glance" in both modes ("Walkthrough practice" during a practice replay, with Return to game above the tabs): week, users, cash and the seed in Challenge Mode; separate Week and Game second values, users and cash in Guided Mode. Do not repeat the paused-time explanation in this summary. The Autosaved tag (or Not saved when the save is blocked) sits in the card footer, alongside the seed note in Challenge Mode. Run holds Challenge Mode's Resume / Save now / How to play / Tutorial and Guided Mode's Resume / Save now / How to play / Exit, followed by the Game mode switch in both modes. Guided Mode's How to play starts a fresh practice company and restores the original company on Finish or Exit walkthrough. Exit uses a door-arrow icon and first attempts to save; a failed save replaces the menu with one confirmation dialog offering Cancel, Save and exit (retry), or Exit without saving. Cancel and Escape restore focus to Exit, and a failed retry leaves the company paused in the dialog. The summary says Not saved when the latest save attempt failed. Sound and Graphics hold the two shared preferences. Saves holds the save files (the pre-update save as a group inside it) on the left and Start over, in a red-bordered box because it replaces the run, on the right; from 760 px wide these are two columns, narrower screens stack them in that order. A Cloud saves section follows below: signed in it carries the same cloud save content as the home screen Account dialog; signed out it explains what an account is for and offers a Go to Account button that switches to the Account tab. Account is its own direct tab in both modes, holding only sign-in for guests and the profile with Sign out once signed in. In both modes a first press on New game or New company turns the button red ("Lose this run? Press again", "Lose this company? Press again") with an "Are you sure?" warning beneath it, and only the second press replaces the run. Playtest opens with an Anonymous analytics section in both modes, whose Opt out toggle (pressed when opted out, like Mute all) stops or resumes analytics at any time; the home screen Account dialog carries the same section. Playtest then holds Give feedback in both modes, followed by Challenge Mode's local playtest data or Guided Mode's local playtest export and observer notes. Feedback opens inline in this tab, keeping one dialog and one keyboard focus boundary. Only the selected tab's sections are in the page, so leaving a tab drops any half-finished confirmation or chosen file. Each section has an icon heading and stacked full-width actions; a confirmation appears beside the control that asked for it. Confirm replacement of saved progress and keep raw export available when a save cannot be read. After a bankrupt campaign the menu opens on Saves, where Export and New company are. Below 760 px the rail gives way to two rows of icon-above-label tabs, so no label shrinks. The sound mute stays next to Menu in all modes and is the same setting as Mute all.
- **Menu type size:** the menu is read at arm's length, so its body text starts at 1 rem (notes and confirmations 1.05 rem), section headings 1.3 rem and buttons 1.15 rem, larger than the rest of the interface. Small uppercase labels are no smaller than 0.85 rem. Raise a menu text size before shrinking a layout to fit.
- **Graphics quality:** Auto, High, Medium and Low change only how the 3D room is drawn, never the interface, labels or game rules. Auto is the default and means Medium on a graphics card and the lightest settings when the browser draws on the CPU; a player with the graphics card for it can choose High. On a graphics card Auto also watches the frame rate: if it falls, Auto draws fewer pixels (never below three quarters) without a new canvas; it never changes shadows, detail or effects by itself, and a quality the player picks is never adapted. High is HD materials, ambient shading, glow and soft 4096 shadows; Medium keeps HD materials and 2048 shadows but drops the glow and ambient pass and draws at the screen's normal pixel size; Low is the flat pixel look, no shadows, three quarters of the pixels and 30 frames a second. The profiles live in `scene/quality.ts`. A change starts a new canvas, which returns the camera to its starting view; say so in the menu.

- **Challenge Mode operations:** permanent advice, warnings and work occupy a flush-left, 280 px sidebar, with separators on one paper surface and no floating-card shadows. The room resizes into the remaining column. **Next move** keeps its existing action; **Warnings** keeps its evidence links. **In progress** shows task progress, weeks left, assigned engineers, unstaffed work and finished releases ready to ship; selecting work opens Team. Testing/fixing replaces its release in the list. Weekly work stays visible but paused during incidents. On screens at or below 900 px, Operations becomes a flush bottom dock above the time controls, collapsed initially with task/warning counts. The expanded dock, including its toggle row, uses at most 30% of the viewport. Its details scroll within that budget and the room resizes vertically; it never overlays the room. The tutorial opens its advice target automatically.

## Player feedback

## Phase 1 adaptations

Call physical simulation time **game seconds** throughout the interface: activation delays, evidence, recovery and chart axes. The total counter does not reset at week end. Every 60 game seconds settles an operating week: credit pending successful-request revenue, deduct accrued running costs and salaries, and record the result in History. Running continues unless another existing pause condition applies; non-positive cash after settlement closes the company. Weeks are compressed accounting periods, not real calendar weeks or chapter completion gates. Preserve internal field names, save formats and seeded timing.

Chapter 0 is a nonmodal card floating beside the playable game. Everything except the current lesson's control is dimmed; the control sits in an undimmed window ringed in the warm gold spotlight colour (never the lavender selection colour), with a short label such as Click or Press Run pinned to it. The dim never takes clicks. Reading lessons with nothing to point at dim the whole game and centre the card. Place the card beside its target so it never covers it; below 900 px it docks to the top or bottom edge away from the target and keeps its reading compact. Highlight and reveal the actual game control automatically. Required exercises use the real evidence buttons, Run, purchase quote and Cancel, and speed selector. Run, the quote, Cancel and speed open the next lesson as soon as they are done, without a pause or message; this waits while Menu is open. The two inspections wait for Next instead: once a component is selected, its readings are lit with its button and kept clear of the card so the player can read the numbers. A lesson revisited with Back keeps its completion and offers Next instead. The Run lesson advances the real company by exactly one game second and pauses automatically; the remaining lessons hold the clock until Finish. Quotes use the current company's real costs, not a practice purchase model. Keyboard focus can move between guide and game; Tab from the lesson heading reaches its control, and each actual modal retains its own focus boundary. The speed exercise opens the real dock menu; the lit window grows to include its open panel, and floors and the camera stay usable there. Examples are labelled and contain no pretend game controls. Back persists the current position and preserves completed exercises within an attempt. Explicit How to play or Replay walkthrough starts a fresh attempt: clear earlier acknowledgements and saved furthest progress and require the real exercises again. For a player who already completed Chapter 0, use a fresh practice company with the same real controls and simulation, labelled Practice in the card. Keep the original company paused in its own save; Finish or Exit walkthrough restores it, including an active incident. Persist the practice attempt separately for reload and Save and exit. First-time Chapter 0 continues to teach on the actual company. Save and exit resumes the unfinished guide. Optional Challenge Mode and later chapter spotlight guides share the card placement and keep their existing presentation.

Reuse the incident workspace, but use **Stable steps: n/5** and a stability meter instead of Challenge Mode's outage deadline. Explain the qualifying latency/error thresholds in the tooltip. Activation delays use physical steps. Inspection is free, immediate and repeatable; do not copy Challenge Mode's investigation timer or disable previously inspected equipment.

Show latency, service errors and total backlog as the compact symptom summary. Full system evidence must retain incoming/admitted/rejected demand; per-component demand, capacity, demand/capacity, busy utilisation, backlog, processed and failed counts; and installed versus routed server capacity. Cash and pending revenue stay in the top stats; the panel has no separate finances disclosure. Equipment readings lead with a short list of icon rows, the exact value on the right, toned green, amber or red; App Server and Database add demand against capacity as a load bar that is green, amber from 85% and red at capacity. Each App Server is a card with a routing status dot, its own load bar and its processed and backlog counts. Purchase previews show Setup, Cash after, Upkeep and Ready in as a 2 × 2 grid of tiles: red spending, green cash left (amber when the warning applies), amber upkeep and blue timing. Guided Mode actions lead with the shared icon for what they change. Inspect equipment starts directly with the component buttons: no request-path line and no separate pause button, since inspection is free and Run/Pause sits in the bottom bar.
Each campaign action shows what it changes in the room instead of in words: while the player points at or focuses an action, or its confirmation is open, the affected machine's marker and floor pad glow blue (Database for a database upgrade, Network for traffic limits and load balancing, Read Cache for cache work, Monitoring for Health Checks, the App Servers otherwise), and when that machine is on the other floor its floor button glows instead.
The panel lists each action only by name, price and activation; its effect and costs are not repeated there.
Pressing an action never buys it straight away. The view rides to the affected machine's floor and a confirmation opens over the room beside that machine's marker, with a notch pointing at it; a Scale up action points at its own App Server.
The camera eases to centre that marker in the part of the room the panel leaves open, at the default zoom whether the player had zoomed in or out; panning, zooming or dragging stops it.
The confirmation uses warm paper, a softly rounded outline and a plum primary button, matching other Warm Classic panels. Equipment targeting and the anchored notch retain their existing meaning.
The confirmation leads with the action and one short effect sentence, followed by the four quote tiles. Keep a routing requirement visible once when adding a server. How it works contains short labelled facts about the equipment and timing, plus any distinct action detail; show the forecast caveat only when a forecast exists. Do not repeat the delay, effect or routing note. Keep No refunds beside Confirm (focused) and Cancel in the pinned footer.
It follows the marker as the camera moves, sits on the marker's left, away from the panel, unless that runs off the room, when it takes the right, and when space is short the preview body scrolls while Confirm and Cancel stay pinned at its foot. Expanded details scroll into view and retain at least three readable lines on the supported short-phone viewport rather than collapsing beneath the warning.
Without a drawn room it settles in the open part of the stage.
Escape or Cancel closes it and returns focus to the action. Only one confirmation is open at a time, and Confirm follows the action's own disabled rule.

The UI reads the simulation snapshot and routes decisions through the store. It must not compute a second outcome, reveal a scripted root cause, recommend the matching fix, or restore weekly/timeout mechanics to make the layout look familiar. Costs, delays, unavailable actions and pending progress must reflect the existing rules. Use player-facing names such as “Database upgrade”, never internal IDs such as `upgrade-db`.

## Phase 4 data strategy

Keep the current campaign stage in the HUD while evidence scrolls. Keep Chapter requirements as an always-visible checklist under the stage guidance, with its completed count. The checklist stays expanded inside the equipment workspace; the workspace itself can collapse. Avoid generic messages telling the player to wait for a growth event. Equipment tooltips give a short role summary; longer explanations stay in the inspector's About disclosure. Continue to Data Strategy is explicit and leaves the company paused.

Reveal the Read Cache inspection chip and room label only after data entry. They select the same component through the existing dispatcher. The request path includes optional Read Cache; nearby evidence explains that eligible misses, writes and non-cacheable reads still reach the database. A cache is not an independent queue or failure source.

Until actual workload growth, show “Waiting for workload growth.” Once applied, show the observed profile/mix, rate used, hits, misses and DB pressure in a compact callout. Keep logical operations, warmth used/after, read/write demand and historical details in disclosures. History with no data observations says the detail was not recorded. Cache/tuning use the same action rows and cost/delay badges as other investments; unavailable database tiers do not advertise a locked purchase.

Cost and delay badges wrap inside the button; all three Opening responses remain reachable in the same panel, including their labels and badges, with vertical scrolling when the selected readings need more room.

Show actual health, detected health, configured routing and effective traffic recipients separately. A detected failed app can remain configured while receiving no traffic; labels must follow the effective recipients. Free per-app health inspection uses the same selection dispatcher as the room and inspector. Research ownership does not create equipment: a reserved spare is one existing installed rack, shown once with its standby state.

The technology tree opens from the Tech view tab into the shared view sheet, with the existing nine-node layout, in both modes. On desktop all nine nodes fit beside the detail panel, including beside Challenge Mode's Operations rail; on touch, retain the stacked detail and horizontally scrollable map. Guided Mode's detail panel uses Challenge Mode's layout (status tag, icon heading, chips, "Needs …" prerequisite links and action rows) with research points and physical-step costs; tooltips explain physical-step costs and effects, and legacy engineer-week descriptions do not describe Guided Mode rules. ## Responsive and accessible behaviour

Use the existing desktop side panel and mobile bottom panel. At 900 px and below, the header wraps and metric tiles retain their icons with compact values. App Health keeps Healthy on one line and wraps longer statuses within its card; its icon yields width before the text. At 360px and below, all four headline metrics share the compact `xs` text token. Purchase preview bodies scroll on short phones, with a readable expanded details area above the fixed warning and decision footer. At 640 px and below, the dock uses two rows: Time above, labeled view tabs and Navigation below. The speed selector remains available. The room stays usable above the mobile panel. Evidence tables may scroll inside their disclosure; the page must not overflow horizontally. Never show scrollbars anywhere in the game: `index.css` hides them globally (`scrollbar-width: none` and `::-webkit-scrollbar`) while wheel, touch, keyboard and trackpad scrolling keep working. Do not restore them with `scrollbar-width`, `scrollbar-color`, `scrollbar-gutter` or `::-webkit-scrollbar` styles on individual panels; indicate more content with layout, such as a clipped next row or a pinned footer. Do not make tables or explanatory paragraphs push every decision far below the initial viewport.

At desktop size, verify all three opening responses fit in the initial incident panel. On mobile, scrolling within the panel is expected; verify equipment selection, each response, detailed evidence, pause, history and Menu remain reachable by touch. Expanded evidence must not cover the footer. Keep tap targets consistent with the existing buttons, visible focus, named controls, `aria-pressed` on selection/toggles, labelled charts and progress bars, and status announcements for inspection and pending actions. Do not rely on colour alone. Preserve reduced-motion styling. Tooltips open on hover (after a short pause) and on keyboard focus, close on Escape, and explain locked controls too. On touch a tap on a term opens its tooltip; tapping a button only acts.

## Review before changing UI

Compare the result with the current committed game's UI and this guide, not only with a standalone mockup. Existing visual references are in [the prototype migration notes](prototype-migration.md); source components and current browser renders are the maintained baseline.

Inspect title, healthy management, active incident, selected equipment, pending response, recovery report, history and Menu on desktop (1440 × 900) and a narrow touch viewport (about 390 px wide). Also check an unavailable action and long/large values. Confirm that warnings, control order, icon meanings and selected states remain familiar, exact evidence remains available, and actions have not gained or lost gameplay effects.

Check button dimensions and neighbouring control positions before and after interaction and asynchronous loading, including delayed account data, long player names and changing labels or counts. Width and height must remain stable through idle, loading, disabled, active, success and error states wherever those states apply.

Follow [testing.md](testing.md) for static checks and the real WebGL browser suite. Extend a browser journey for changed selection, disclosure or action behaviour; take screenshots for visual inspection. Passing tests establish wiring and reachability, not visual consistency by themselves. Include any intentional design departure and its gameplay reason in the PR description, and update this guide when that departure is approved.
