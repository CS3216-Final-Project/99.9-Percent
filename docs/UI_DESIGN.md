# UI design for 99.99%

This guide records the current playable game's design. It applies to Campaign and Classic, including later phases. Gameplay rules may change; the shared visual language and interaction patterns should remain familiar. The proposal's future 2D direction is planning context and does not replace the current room or UI without a separate design decision.

## Visual language and reusable components

The isometric room is the main canvas. A dark purple HUD frames cream paper panels, outlined pixel icons and yellow primary actions. Use the tokens in `frontend/src/index.css` for colours, fonts, borders, radii, spacing and shadows. Pixelify Sans is the display face; Rubik is the body and numeric face. Numbers use tabular figures. Do not introduce a separate theme or a compressed text-only HUD for a new mode.

The room itself is a realistic office, apart from the HUD's purple: warm off-white walls, concrete, grey carpet tiles where the crew work and coloured carpets in the rooms, charcoal and grey metal racks, white and light grey furniture, with a few colour accents. Its colours live in `frontend/src/components/scene/palette.ts` (`ROOM_PALETTE`, `FURNITURE`, `EQUIPMENT`, `PIXEL_FLOORS`); take new room colours from there rather than writing hex values into the scene. The HUD tokens in `index.css` are for the interface only.

## Screen structure

- **Home screen:** one screen for every visit, whichever mode was played last: a first visit, a Campaign in progress and a Classic in progress look the same. The room remains behind the overlay. The title kicker and the account chip share the top row, then the game name and tag line, then two equally prominent Campaign and Classic cards under “Choose how you want to play”, stacked on narrow screens. Each card has its pace line, a short explanation (continuous guided infrastructure challenges versus the week-by-week 50,000-user, 26-week business challenge), a saved-game box and its own primary button. The saved-game box reads “Chapter n of 5: name” for Campaign and “Week n of 26” for Classic, or “No saved game”; phones hide the empty box so both Play buttons show first. The button is Play Campaign / Continue company and Play Classic / Continue week n; Classic in progress adds New game, and Replay the tutorial once the tutorial is done. The mode played last gets a yellow “Last played” tag and keyboard focus on its button, but no mode is recommended. Choosing the other mode switches to it at once and keeps the first mode's save; when the current run cannot be saved, the button turns red as “Switch and lose this run” with a warning, as in the menu. No run starts before a choice. Give feedback sits below the cards.
- **Account:** the chip reads Sign in (with “Save to the cloud”) for guests and shows the player's initials and name once signed in. It opens one Account dialog. Signed out, the dialog explains what an account is for, then shows Continue with Google, an “or use your email” divider and an email form that switches between Sign in and Create an account (optional name, password at least 8 characters with a show/hide toggle). Errors appear under the fields and keep what was typed. When accounts are offline, a warning callout with Try again replaces the status line and every option stays usable. Signed in, the dialog shows the profile with Sign out, then “This browser” (the Campaign save and Save to cloud, or why there is nothing to save), “In your account” (cloud saves with their time and version, each with Load, plus Refresh) and a Backup disclosure. Loading a cloud save closes the dialog and returns to the home screen with that save ready to continue. Say “cloud save” and “local save”, never “cloud company”. Menu, Saves shows the same content inline under an “Account and cloud saves” heading.
- **Top bar:** the brand at the left, four familiar metric tiles, then the sound mute and Menu. The week or incident clock is not here: it sits in the bottom bar beside the buttons that advance it, so the player does not look across the screen between reading the time and moving it on. Health changes to the incident icon and the whole header gets the red striped treatment during an incident. Campaign pending revenue must be clearly distinguished from available cash and settled revenue.
- **Campaign chapters:** during play, a dedicated bar below the top stats shows “Campaign · Chapter n of 5” and the current chapter name in display type. On wide screens, show a five-chapter trail with completed/current/available/locked labels; on narrower screens keep the current chapter and View chapters button visible. View chapters opens a paused overview with all five names, short descriptions and statuses; closing leaves the company paused. This overview does not skip chapters or change progression. It stays outside the scrollable evidence panel; detailed requirements remain in that panel’s Campaign progression disclosure.
- **Management:** room equipment is clickable. Selecting it gives immediate visible feedback with its name, icon and authoritative metrics. Campaign can keep a compact system overview visible because only a few opening actions exist. Outside incidents the stage objective leads the overview; during an incident it follows the responses. Response rows show the name, then cost and delay badges underneath, so the narrow panel never scrolls horizontally. At 1440 × 900 the healthy overview fits without scrolling when its disclosures are closed.
- **Incident:** retain the red banner and symptom-focused title, a compact progress area, three impact tiles, then the numbered jobs **1. Inspect the evidence** and **2. Choose a response**. Equipment controls mirror the room. Selected equipment is highlighted and its latest observation is shown. Keep the permitted responses equally prominent; no full metrics table before the response list.
- **Bottom bar:** view tabs on the left, in the order Tech, Team, History: Tech and History in both modes, Team only in Classic, each with its icon, name and count badge. On the right the time readout, then the play/pause icon, selected speed and primary advance action. The readout is Classic's "Week n/26" with its 26-block progress track, Campaign's "Week · Step", or "Incident" with its clock, and it takes the red blinking icon during an incident. Below 640 px the readout takes its own row above the tabs and buttons, with the track filling the row, rather than squeezing the advance button. Keep accessible names for icon-only buttons. Use labels that match the mode's actual time model.
- **Review:** report in a shared modal with outcome tag, time range, spending and evidence-based explanations. Raw snapshots belong in a disclosure. Continue returns to the same company.
- **Views:** Tech, Team and History open in the same night sheet over the room in both modes, titled "Tech tree", "Team" and "History". The sheet covers the room and side panel but leaves the top and bottom bars; the active tab stays pressed. Closing it, or Esc, returns to the room.
- **History:** the same three tabs in both modes: **Charts**, **Events** and **Incidents (n)**. Use shared chart cards with physical steps or weeks labelled accurately; Campaign charts latency and traffic by step and revenue and costs by operating week. Events is the shared feed, newest first, labelled with the mode's time unit. Incidents lists each postmortem as a row that expands to the report; Campaign's stage outcomes sit above them on paper cards. Financial settlements and event details can use disclosures.
- **Menu:** the same five tabs, in the same order, in both modes: **Run, Sound, Graphics, Saves, Playtest**. Above the tabs stays what must be seen whichever tab is open: the run card and a big Resume in both modes, and any unreadable-save warning. The card is "This run" (week, users, cash and the seed) in Classic and "This company" (week and step, users, cash and the paused notice) in Campaign, with an Autosaved tag, or Not saved when the save is blocked. Run holds the things done during play as the same big tiles: Classic's Save now / How to play / Tutorial, Campaign's Save now / How to play (the introduction) / Save and exit, and in both the Game mode switch. Sound and Graphics hold the two shared preferences. Saves holds the save files (the pre-update save as a group inside it) and Campaign's Account and cloud saves section on the left, and Start over, in a red-bordered box because it replaces the run, on the right; from 760 px wide these are two columns, narrower screens stack them in that order. In both modes a first press on New game or New company turns the button red ("Lose this run? Press again", "Lose this company? Press again") with an "Are you sure?" warning beneath it, and only the second press replaces the run. Playtest holds Give feedback in both modes, followed by Classic's local playtest data or Campaign's local playtest export and observer notes. Feedback opens inline in this tab, keeping one dialog and one keyboard focus boundary. Only the selected tab's sections are in the page, so leaving a tab drops any half-finished confirmation or chosen file. Each section has an icon heading and stacked full-width actions; a confirmation appears beside the control that asked for it. Confirm replacement of saved progress and keep raw export available when a save cannot be read. After a bankrupt campaign the menu opens on Saves, where Export and New company are. Below 760 px the strip becomes two rows of icon-above-label tabs, so no label shrinks. The sound mute stays next to Menu in all modes and is the same setting as Mute all.
- **Menu type size:** the menu is read at arm's length, so its body text starts at 1 rem (notes and confirmations 1.05 rem), section headings 1.3 rem and buttons 1.15 rem, larger than the rest of the interface. Small uppercase labels are no smaller than 0.85 rem. Raise a menu text size before shrinking a layout to fit.
- **Graphics quality:** Auto, High, Medium and Low change only how the 3D room is drawn, never the interface, labels or game rules. Auto is the default and means High on a graphics card and the lightest settings when the browser draws on the CPU. On a graphics card Auto also watches the frame rate: if it falls, Auto draws fewer pixels (never below three quarters) and then drops the glow and ambient pass, both without a new canvas; it never changes shadows or detail by itself, and a quality the player picks is never adapted. High is HD materials, ambient shading, glow and soft 4096 shadows; Medium keeps HD materials and 2048 shadows but drops the glow and ambient pass and draws at the screen's normal pixel size; Low is the flat pixel look, no shadows, three quarters of the pixels and 30 frames a second. The profiles live in `scene/quality.ts`. A change starts a new canvas, which returns the camera to its starting view; say so in the menu.

- **Classic operations:** permanent advice, warnings and work occupy a flush-left, 280 px sidebar, with separators on one paper surface and no floating-card shadows. The room resizes into the remaining column. **Next move** keeps its existing action; **Warnings** keeps its evidence links. **In progress** shows task progress, weeks left, assigned engineers, unstaffed work and finished releases ready to ship; selecting work opens Team. Testing/fixing replaces its release in the list. Weekly work stays visible but paused during incidents. On screens at or below 900 px, Operations becomes a flush bottom dock above the time controls, collapsed initially with task/warning counts. Expanding it uses at most 30% of the viewport for scrollable details and resizes the room vertically; it never overlays the room. The tutorial opens its advice target automatically.

## Player feedback

Replace the title's Join Playtest registration link with **Give feedback**. Use the shared paper modal on title screens and an inline form in Menu → Playtest. Label the textarea **Your feedback**, limit it to 2,000 characters, and submit only when the player chooses **Send feedback**. No sign-in is required. Upload only the submitted message, current mode and a random submission ID; do not attach save files or local playtest records. Confirm success only after database persistence. Keep text after errors, disable duplicate sends while pending, and preserve the same ID when retrying unchanged text. Closing cancels the request; title-screen feedback never starts or advances the game.

## Phase 1 adaptations

Reuse the incident workspace, but use **Stable steps: n/5** and a stability meter instead of Classic's outage deadline. Explain the qualifying latency/error thresholds in the tooltip. Activation delays use physical steps. Inspection is free, immediate and repeatable; do not copy Classic's investigation timer or disable previously inspected equipment.

Show latency, service errors and total backlog as the compact symptom summary. Full system evidence must retain incoming/admitted/rejected demand; per-component demand, capacity, demand/capacity, busy utilisation, backlog, processed and failed counts; and installed versus routed server capacity. Keep finances separate from system evidence, with cash, pending revenue, unsettled costs and the next settlement boundary. Rejected opportunity value is forgone revenue, not an additional cash charge.

The UI reads the simulation snapshot and routes decisions through the store. It must not compute a second outcome, reveal a scripted root cause, recommend the matching fix, or restore weekly/timeout mechanics to make the layout look familiar. Costs, delays, unavailable actions and pending progress must reflect the existing rules. Use player-facing names such as “Database upgrade”, never internal IDs such as `upgrade-db`.

## Phase 4 data strategy

Keep the current campaign stage in the HUD while evidence scrolls. Detailed progression and next-stage prerequisites use a shared disclosure after the response workspace, so the opening incident's three responses remain visible. Continue to Data Strategy is explicit and leaves the company paused.

Reveal the Read Cache inspection chip and room label only after data entry. They select the same component through the existing dispatcher. The request path includes optional Read Cache; nearby evidence explains that eligible misses, writes and non-cacheable reads still reach the database. A cache is not an independent queue or failure source.

Until actual workload growth, show “Waiting for workload growth.” Once applied, show the observed profile/mix, rate used, hits, misses and DB pressure in a compact callout. Keep logical operations, warmth used/after, read/write demand and historical details in disclosures. History with no data observations says the detail was not recorded. Cache/tuning use the same action rows and cost/delay badges as other investments; unavailable database tiers do not advertise a locked purchase.

## Phase 6 reliability

Keep the same incident workspace and action rows. Reliability responses follow the three shared capacity/admission responses, before older scaling and data controls. Cost and delay badges wrap inside the button; all three Opening responses must remain inside the initial desktop panel, including their labels and badges.

Show actual health, detected health, configured routing and effective traffic recipients separately. A detected failed app can remain configured while receiving no traffic; labels must follow the effective recipients. Free per-app health inspection uses the same selection dispatcher as the room and inspector. Research ownership does not create equipment: a reserved spare is one existing installed rack, shown once with its standby state.

The technology tree opens from the Tech view tab into the shared view sheet, with the existing nine-node layout, in both modes. On desktop all nine nodes fit beside the detail panel, including beside Classic's Operations rail; on touch, retain the stacked detail and horizontally scrollable map. Campaign's detail panel uses Classic's layout (status tag, icon heading, chips, "Needs …" prerequisite links and action rows) with research points and physical-step costs; tooltips explain physical-step costs and effects, and legacy engineer-week descriptions do not describe Campaign rules. Unlocking, deploying and receiving traffic remain distinct states.

Reliability reports explain failure, detection, rerouting, surviving capacity, admitted demand and restoration in player-facing language. Detailed snapshots retain exact evidence. The completion outcome distinguishes an actual incident from a test that opened none, records the admission/cost trade-offs at completion, and remains available in History after acknowledgement.

## Responsive and accessible behaviour

Use the existing desktop side panel and mobile bottom panel. At 900 px and below, the header wraps, metric tiles reduce to icon/headline pairs, view tabs become icons and the speed selector hides. The room stays usable above the mobile panel. Evidence tables may scroll inside their disclosure; the page must not overflow horizontally. Do not make tables or explanatory paragraphs push every decision far below the initial viewport.

At desktop size, verify all three opening responses fit in the initial incident panel. On mobile, scrolling within the panel is expected; verify equipment selection, each response, detailed evidence, pause, history and Menu remain reachable by touch. Expanded evidence must not cover the footer. Keep tap targets consistent with the existing buttons, visible focus, named controls, `aria-pressed` on selection/toggles, labelled charts and progress bars, and status announcements for inspection and pending actions. Do not rely on colour alone. Preserve reduced-motion styling. Tooltips open on hover (after a short pause) and on keyboard focus, close on Escape, and explain locked controls too. On touch a tap on a term opens its tooltip; tapping a button only acts.

## Review before changing UI

Compare the result with the current committed game's UI and this guide, not only with a standalone mockup. Existing visual references are in [the prototype migration notes](prototype-migration.md); source components and current browser renders are the maintained baseline.

Inspect title, healthy management, active incident, selected equipment, pending response, recovery report, history and Menu on desktop (1440 × 900) and a narrow touch viewport (about 390 px wide). Also check an unavailable action and long/large values. Confirm that warnings, control order, icon meanings and selected states remain familiar, exact evidence remains available, and actions have not gained or lost gameplay effects.

Follow [testing.md](testing.md) for static checks and the real WebGL browser suite. Extend a browser journey for changed selection, disclosure or action behaviour; take screenshots for visual inspection. Passing tests establish wiring and reachability, not visual consistency by themselves. Include any intentional design departure and its gameplay reason in the PR description, and update this guide when that departure is approved.
