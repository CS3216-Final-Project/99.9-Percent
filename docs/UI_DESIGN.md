# UI design for 99.99%

This guide records the current playable game's design. It applies to both modes - presented to players as Guided Mode and Challenge Mode - including later phases. Gameplay rules may change; the shared visual language and interaction patterns should remain familiar. The proposal's future 2D direction is planning context and does not replace the current room or UI without a separate design decision.

## Visual language and reusable components

Use the shared font-size constants in `index.css`: `--text-xs` (0.8 rem), `--text-sm` (0.9 rem), `--text-md` (1 rem), `--text-lg` (1.15 rem), `--text-xl` (1.35 rem), `--text-2xl` (1.6 rem), `--text-3xl` (2.1 rem) and `--text-4xl` (2.9 rem). Body text starts at md; use sm and xs for secondary labels and larger sizes for headings and prominent values. Preserve browser zoom. Responsive display titles can interpolate between shared endpoints; ordinary text and controls use the scale rather than one-off sizes.

The isometric room is the main canvas. A dark purple HUD frames cream paper panels, outlined pixel icons and yellow primary actions. Use the tokens in `frontend/src/index.css` for colours, fonts, borders, radii, spacing and shadows. Space Grotesk is the face for the logo, headings, controls, labels, metrics and room lettering; Source Sans 3 is the body face for sentences, longer explanations, tooltips and text entry. These are the only two game typefaces, bundled locally. Numbers use tabular figures. Keep canvas font loading and redraws in sync with the interface fonts so room signs, speech bubbles and billboards use the same typography. Do not introduce a separate theme or a compressed text-only HUD for a new mode.

Buttons must keep the same width and height across their states at a given viewport size. Account for every label, icon, badge and loading indicator before choosing dimensions: clicking, toggling, disabling, loading data or completing a request must not resize a control or move neighbouring controls. Reserve sufficient space from the first render with a shared size or a layout sized for the largest supported state; do not shrink the text to make a later state fit. Responsive sizes may change with the viewport, but state changes must not change the layout. For example, the home-screen Account chip must reserve its final footprint while account data loads and keep it when switching between Sign in and the player's identity; constrain long names within that space while keeping the full name accessible. Apply the same rule to Run/Pause, changing speed labels, counts and pending/success/error button labels.

The room itself is a realistic office, apart from the HUD's purple: warm off-white walls, concrete, grey carpet tiles where the crew work and coloured carpets in the rooms, charcoal and grey metal racks, white and light grey furniture, with a few colour accents. Its colours live in `frontend/src/components/scene/palette.ts` (`ROOM_PALETTE`, `FURNITURE`, `EQUIPMENT`, `PIXEL_FLOORS`); take new room colours from there rather than writing hex values into the scene. The HUD tokens in `index.css` are for the interface only.

The building has two floors on one footprint (`game/floors.ts`, `scene/levels.ts`): the office on top and the server floor below, where every machine stands; the people's desks (Deploy, Team, Growth) stay upstairs. The room shows one floor at a time, and only the floor the elevator stands at has labels and clickable equipment, so nothing on screen points at a floor that is not drawn. The floor menu sits over the camera buttons like an elevator's panel: one button per floor with its storey number, the floor in view pressed and yellow, and a badge on a floor out of view that needs the player (amber for a warning, red and pulsing for an incident, with the reason in the button's accessible name). Switching rides the elevator in 5 seconds: the moving room stays visible through animated shaft shadows and passing landing lights, skipped by a second press, any click or a key, and cut straight through with reduced motion. Choosing equipment from a panel, the inspector or a tutorial step takes the view to its floor; an incident never moves it, but its panel offers Go to the server floor. Page Up and Page Down switch floors except while typing or in a dialog or menu. The floor in view is remembered in this browser, per mode, like the walk mode and camera below it; it is never part of a save.

Walk mode is optional and never the only way to do anything. Its Walk button sits among the camera buttons, pressed and yellow while on, and hands the keys back to the room when pressed. The avatar wears a yellow ring so it stands out from the crew, and a yellow target marks a click-planned destination until it arrives. What it can reach is a button low in the middle of the room ("Space · Inspect Database"), so a tap works on a phone, with the same words in a polite live region for screen readers. Keys belong first to a text field, a dialog or a menu; the arrows also to a focused button, tab, radio or slider, while Space uses what the avatar can reach even then — only with nothing in reach does a focused control keep it. A fun thing the avatar plays with says so in the same button while its animation runs ("Space · Resting in a nap pod…"), and the avatar moves at a brisk walk with Shift to run. Esc closes in a fixed order: a dialog, a view sheet, walk mode, then the selection. Avatars are chosen from pictures in a radio grid, from a chip beside the account's on the home screen or the menu's Avatar tab; the choice is a browser setting, never part of a save.

Each zone of the office has a floor of its own and its name floating over it (`ZONE_NAMES` in `scene/floorplan.ts`, `SERVER_ZONE_NAMES` downstairs): a small dark sign drawn in the 3D scene, always facing the camera and 20 px tall at any zoom, smaller than the equipment's labels and fading out when the camera pulls back too far to read it. Zone names are scenery, not interface: they carry no actions, ignore clicks and stay out of the accessibility tree. Machine rooms keep their equipment labels and get no zone name.

## Screen structure

**Chapter 0: Getting started** integrates Guided Mode's How to play walkthrough into the chapter sequence before the six gameplay chapters (which keep their numbers 1–6). While learning, the chapter bar shows Chapter 0 and its learning goal. View chapters includes the introduction as entry 0 and Replay Chapter 0, which preserves the company and its investments; Menu → How to play replays the same introduction. Teach inspection, demand/capacity/queues, latency and errors, purchase confirmation, game time and weekly accounting, solvency, speed, floors/camera/optional walking, Tech, History, saves/Account, incidents and recovery, and the purpose of later scaling, data, automation, reliability and growth chapters. Detailed new controls are introduced again when their chapter opens. Keep management paused through the explanations; finishing or skipping releases the clock. Preserve lesson resume and already completed profiles.

- **Home screen:** one screen for every visit, whichever mode was played last: a first visit, a Guided Mode run in progress and a Challenge Mode run in progress look the same. The room remains behind the overlay. The title kicker and the account chip share the top row, then the game name and tag line, then the Guided Mode and Challenge Mode cards under “Choose how you want to play”, Guided first, stacked on narrow screens. Each card has its pace line, a short explanation (learning infrastructure concepts through six guided chapters versus independently reaching 50,000 users in the week-by-week 26-week challenge), a saved-game box and its own primary button. The saved-game box reads “Chapter n of 6: name” for Guided Mode and “Week n of 26” for Challenge Mode, or “No saved game”; phones hide the empty box so the start buttons show first. The button is Start Guided Mode / Continue company and Start Challenge Mode / Continue week n; Challenge Mode in progress adds New game. Either card adds a Replay the tutorial link once that mode's walkthrough is done: Challenge Mode's restarts the first week, Guided Mode's runs on the company as it is, and a closed Guided Mode company has none. Challenge Mode is locked until the first five Guided Mode chapters are complete: while locked its card shows the lock with “Complete the first five Guided Mode chapters to unlock.” and a disabled “Complete Guided Mode to unlock” button instead of a start control. The mode played last gets a yellow “Last played” tag and keyboard focus on its button. Choosing the other mode switches to it at once and keeps the first mode's save; when the current run cannot be saved, the button turns red as “Switch and lose this run” with a warning, as in the menu. No run starts before a choice. Below the cards a sequencing note explains that Guided Mode comes first and each mode keeps its own save. Anonymous analytics is on by default. Until the player answers, an analytics notice (“Help improve 99.99%”) sits below the mode selection, is kept compact (text on the left, buttons on the right, stacked on phones), shows “99.99%” in the logo face, explains what is tracked, that nothing is sent while the browser's Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control setting is on, and offers a yellow “OK” and a red “Opt out”; either choice is remembered and the panel does not return. Give feedback sits below.
- **Account:** the chip reads Sign in (with “Save to the cloud”) for guests and shows the player's initials and name once signed in. It opens one Account dialog. Signed out, the dialog explains what an account is for, then shows Continue with Google, an “or use your email” divider and an email form that switches between Sign in and Create an account (optional name, password at least 8 characters with a show/hide toggle). Errors appear under the fields and keep what was typed. When accounts are offline, a warning callout with Try again replaces the status line and every option stays usable. Signed in, the dialog shows the profile with Sign out, then “This browser” (the Guided Mode save and Save to cloud, or why there is nothing to save), “In your account” (cloud saves with their time and version, each with Load, plus Refresh) and a Backup disclosure. Loading a cloud save closes the dialog and returns to the home screen with that save ready to continue. Say “cloud save” and “local save”, never “cloud company”.
- **Top bar:** the brand at the left, four familiar metric tiles, then the sound mute and Menu. The week or incident clock is not here: it sits in the bottom bar beside the buttons that advance it, so the player does not look across the screen between reading the time and moving it on. Health changes to the incident icon and the whole header gets the red striped treatment during an incident. Guided Mode pending revenue must be clearly distinguished from available cash and settled revenue.
- **Guided Mode chapters:** a compact bar below the company stats shows the current chapter number and name, one state-derived goal, Next when offered by the existing progression gates, and the existing View chapters button at the right. On phones the goal wraps and Next takes its own full-width row. The Chapter 0 introduction and six gameplay chapters live in the paused View chapters overview. Closing restores focus and leaves time paused. A disabled Next has a visible, keyboard-reachable explanation. Keep detailed requirements, pending actions, stability, reviews and warnings in System Overview; do not repeat its generic objective there.
- **Management:** room equipment is clickable. Selecting it gives immediate visible feedback with its name, icon and authoritative metrics. Guided Mode can keep a compact system overview visible because only a few opening actions exist. The chapter bar carries the objective; the overview retains specific next actions and requirements. Response rows show the name, then cost and delay badges underneath, so the narrow panel never scrolls horizontally. At 1440 × 900 the healthy overview fits without scrolling when its disclosures are closed.
- **Incident:** retain the red banner and symptom-focused title, a compact progress area, three impact tiles, then the numbered jobs **1. Inspect the evidence** and **2. Choose a response**. Equipment controls mirror the room. Selected equipment is highlighted and its latest observation is shown. Keep the permitted responses equally prominent; no full metrics table before the response list.
- **Bottom bar:** view tabs on the left, in the order Tech, Team, History: Tech and History in both modes, Team only in Challenge Mode, each with its icon, name and count badge. On the right the time readout, then the play/pause icon, selected speed and primary advance action. The readout is Challenge Mode's "Week n/26" with its 26-block progress track, Guided Mode's "Week · Step", or "Incident" with its clock, and it takes the red blinking icon during an incident. Below 640 px the readout takes its own row above the tabs and buttons, with the track filling the row, rather than squeezing the advance button. Keep accessible names for icon-only buttons. Use labels that match the mode's actual time model.
- **Review:** report in a shared modal with outcome tag, time range, spending and evidence-based explanations. Raw snapshots belong in a disclosure. Continue returns to the same company.
- **Views:** Tech, Team and History open in the same night sheet over the room in both modes, titled "Tech tree", "Team" and "History". The sheet covers the room and side panel but leaves the top and bottom bars; the active tab stays pressed. Closing it, or Esc, returns to the room.
- **History:** the same three tabs in both modes: **Charts**, **Events** and **Incidents (n)**. Use shared chart cards with physical steps or weeks labelled accurately; Guided Mode charts latency and traffic by step and revenue and costs by operating week. Events is the shared feed, newest first, labelled with the mode's time unit. Incidents lists each postmortem as a row that expands to the report; Guided Mode's stage outcomes sit above them on paper cards. Financial settlements and event details can use disclosures.
- **Menu:** the same seven tabs, in the same order, in both modes: **Run, Sound, Graphics, Avatar, Saves, Account, Playtest**. From 760 px the tabs form a stable left rail beside the panel. Above the tabs stays what must be seen whichever tab is open: the run card and a big Resume in both modes, and any unreadable-save warning. The card is "This run" (week, users, cash and the seed) in Challenge Mode and "This company" (week and step, users, cash and the paused notice) in Guided Mode, with an Autosaved tag, or Not saved when the save is blocked. Run holds the things done during play as the same big tiles: Challenge Mode's Save now / How to play / Tutorial, Guided Mode's Save now / How to play (replays the walkthrough on the same company) / Save and exit, and in both the Game mode switch. Sound and Graphics hold the two shared preferences. Saves holds the save files (the pre-update save as a group inside it) on the left and Start over, in a red-bordered box because it replaces the run, on the right; from 760 px wide these are two columns, narrower screens stack them in that order. A Cloud saves section follows below: signed in it carries the same cloud save content as the home screen Account dialog; signed out it explains what an account is for and offers a Go to Account button that switches to the Account tab. Account is its own direct tab in both modes, holding only sign-in for guests and the profile with Sign out once signed in. In both modes a first press on New game or New company turns the button red ("Lose this run? Press again", "Lose this company? Press again") with an "Are you sure?" warning beneath it, and only the second press replaces the run. Playtest opens with an Anonymous analytics section in both modes, whose Opt out toggle (pressed when opted out, like Mute all) stops or resumes analytics at any time; the home screen Account dialog carries the same section. Playtest then holds Give feedback in both modes, followed by Challenge Mode's local playtest data or Guided Mode's local playtest export and observer notes. Feedback opens inline in this tab, keeping one dialog and one keyboard focus boundary. Only the selected tab's sections are in the page, so leaving a tab drops any half-finished confirmation or chosen file. Each section has an icon heading and stacked full-width actions; a confirmation appears beside the control that asked for it. Confirm replacement of saved progress and keep raw export available when a save cannot be read. After a bankrupt campaign the menu opens on Saves, where Export and New company are. Below 760 px the rail gives way to two rows of icon-above-label tabs, so no label shrinks. The sound mute stays next to Menu in all modes and is the same setting as Mute all.
- **Menu type size:** the menu is read at arm's length, so its body text starts at 1 rem (notes and confirmations 1.05 rem), section headings 1.3 rem and buttons 1.15 rem, larger than the rest of the interface. Small uppercase labels are no smaller than 0.85 rem. Raise a menu text size before shrinking a layout to fit.
- **Graphics quality:** Auto, High, Medium and Low change only how the 3D room is drawn, never the interface, labels or game rules. Auto is the default and means Medium on a graphics card and the lightest settings when the browser draws on the CPU; a player with the graphics card for it can choose High. On a graphics card Auto also watches the frame rate: if it falls, Auto draws fewer pixels (never below three quarters) without a new canvas; it never changes shadows, detail or effects by itself, and a quality the player picks is never adapted. High is HD materials, ambient shading, glow and soft 4096 shadows; Medium keeps HD materials and 2048 shadows but drops the glow and ambient pass and draws at the screen's normal pixel size; Low is the flat pixel look, no shadows, three quarters of the pixels and 30 frames a second. The profiles live in `scene/quality.ts`. A change starts a new canvas, which returns the camera to its starting view; say so in the menu.

- **Challenge Mode operations:** permanent advice, warnings and work occupy a flush-left, 280 px sidebar, with separators on one paper surface and no floating-card shadows. The room resizes into the remaining column. **Next move** keeps its existing action; **Warnings** keeps its evidence links. **In progress** shows task progress, weeks left, assigned engineers, unstaffed work and finished releases ready to ship; selecting work opens Team. Testing/fixing replaces its release in the list. Weekly work stays visible but paused during incidents. On screens at or below 900 px, Operations becomes a flush bottom dock above the time controls, collapsed initially with task/warning counts. Expanding it uses at most 30% of the viewport for scrollable details and resizes the room vertically; it never overlays the room. The tutorial opens its advice target automatically.

## Player feedback

Replace the title's Join Playtest registration link with **Give feedback**. Use the shared paper modal on title screens and an inline form in Menu → Playtest. Label the textarea **Your feedback**, limit it to 2,000 characters, and submit only when the player chooses **Send feedback**. No sign-in is required. Upload only the submitted message, current mode and a random submission ID; do not attach save files or local playtest records. Confirm success only after database persistence. Keep text after errors, disable duplicate sends while pending, and preserve the same ID when retrying unchanged text. Closing cancels the request; title-screen feedback never starts or advances the game.

## Phase 1 adaptations

Call physical simulation time **game seconds** throughout the interface: activation delays, evidence, recovery and chart axes. The total counter does not reset at week end. Every 60 game seconds settles an operating week: credit pending successful-request revenue, deduct accrued running costs and salaries, and record the result in History. Running continues unless another existing pause condition applies; non-positive cash after settlement closes the company. Weeks are compressed accounting periods, not real calendar weeks or chapter completion gates. Preserve internal field names, save formats and seeded timing.

Chapter 0 cards lead with the highlighted instruction, followed by a short explanation and a visible lesson counter. Keep interaction-based completion for free inspections and the purchase preview; inspection reveals provide the evidence after selecting equipment. Reserve the Next/Finish button footprint for the longest label. The clock introduction describes Run after the guide. Slow response time and failed requests are separate warning measurements. Challenge Mode keeps its existing tutorial presentation.

Reuse the incident workspace, but use **Stable steps: n/5** and a stability meter instead of Challenge Mode's outage deadline. Explain the qualifying latency/error thresholds in the tooltip. Activation delays use physical steps. Inspection is free, immediate and repeatable; do not copy Challenge Mode's investigation timer or disable previously inspected equipment.

Show latency, service errors and total backlog as the compact symptom summary. Full system evidence must retain incoming/admitted/rejected demand; per-component demand, capacity, demand/capacity, busy utilisation, backlog, processed and failed counts; and installed versus routed server capacity. Cash and pending revenue stay in the top stats; the panel has no separate finances disclosure. System evidence starts directly with the component buttons: no request-path line and no separate pause button, since inspection is free and Run/Pause sits in the bottom bar.
Each campaign action shows what it changes in the room instead of in words: while the player points at or focuses an action, or its confirmation is open, the affected machine's marker and floor pad glow blue (Database for a database upgrade, Network for traffic limits and load balancing, Read Cache for cache work, Monitoring for Health Checks, the App Servers otherwise), and when that machine is on the other floor its floor button glows instead.
The panel lists each action only by name, price and activation; its effect and costs are not repeated there.
Pressing an action never buys it straight away. The view rides to the affected machine's floor and a confirmation opens over the room beside that machine's marker, with a notch pointing at it; a Scale up action points at its own App Server.
The camera eases to centre that marker in the part of the room the panel leaves open, at the default zoom whether the player had zoomed in or out; panning, zooming or dragging stops it.
The confirmation wears the night colours of the header and the room's overlays, with a blue edge, the machine name in blue and the action title in yellow.
The confirmation names the machine and says in one sentence what it does, then gives the action as its title, the action's effect, Setup, Cash after, upkeep and Activation as label/value rows, the no-refund note, and Confirm (focused) and Cancel.
It follows the marker as the camera moves, sits on the marker's left, away from the panel, unless that runs off the room, when it takes the right, and on a short room scrolls its details while Confirm and Cancel stay pinned at its foot.
Without a drawn room it settles in the open part of the stage.
Escape or Cancel closes it and returns focus to the action. Only one confirmation is open at a time, and Confirm follows the action's own disabled rule.

The UI reads the simulation snapshot and routes decisions through the store. It must not compute a second outcome, reveal a scripted root cause, recommend the matching fix, or restore weekly/timeout mechanics to make the layout look familiar. Costs, delays, unavailable actions and pending progress must reflect the existing rules. Use player-facing names such as “Database upgrade”, never internal IDs such as `upgrade-db`.

## Phase 4 data strategy

Keep the current campaign stage in the HUD while evidence scrolls. Next-stage prerequisites use the Chapter requirements disclosure under the stage guidance, so the opening incident's three responses remain visible. Continue to Data Strategy is explicit and leaves the company paused.

Reveal the Read Cache inspection chip and room label only after data entry. They select the same component through the existing dispatcher. The request path includes optional Read Cache; nearby evidence explains that eligible misses, writes and non-cacheable reads still reach the database. A cache is not an independent queue or failure source.

Until actual workload growth, show “Waiting for workload growth.” Once applied, show the observed profile/mix, rate used, hits, misses and DB pressure in a compact callout. Keep logical operations, warmth used/after, read/write demand and historical details in disclosures. History with no data observations says the detail was not recorded. Cache/tuning use the same action rows and cost/delay badges as other investments; unavailable database tiers do not advertise a locked purchase.

## Phase 6 reliability

Keep the same incident workspace and action rows. Reliability responses follow the three shared capacity/admission responses, before older scaling and data controls. Cost and delay badges wrap inside the button; all three Opening responses must remain inside the initial desktop panel, including their labels and badges.

Show actual health, detected health, configured routing and effective traffic recipients separately. A detected failed app can remain configured while receiving no traffic; labels must follow the effective recipients. Free per-app health inspection uses the same selection dispatcher as the room and inspector. Research ownership does not create equipment: a reserved spare is one existing installed rack, shown once with its standby state.

The technology tree opens from the Tech view tab into the shared view sheet, with the existing nine-node layout, in both modes. On desktop all nine nodes fit beside the detail panel, including beside Challenge Mode's Operations rail; on touch, retain the stacked detail and horizontally scrollable map. Guided Mode's detail panel uses Challenge Mode's layout (status tag, icon heading, chips, "Needs …" prerequisite links and action rows) with research points and physical-step costs; tooltips explain physical-step costs and effects, and legacy engineer-week descriptions do not describe Guided Mode rules. Unlocking, deploying and receiving traffic remain distinct states.

Reliability reports explain failure, detection, rerouting, surviving capacity, admitted demand and restoration in player-facing language. Detailed snapshots retain exact evidence. The completion outcome distinguishes an actual incident from a test that opened none, records the admission/cost trade-offs at completion, and remains available in History after acknowledgement.

## Responsive and accessible behaviour

Use the existing desktop side panel and mobile bottom panel. At 900 px and below, the header wraps, metric tiles reduce to icon/headline pairs, view tabs become icons and the speed selector hides. The room stays usable above the mobile panel. Evidence tables may scroll inside their disclosure; the page must not overflow horizontally. Do not make tables or explanatory paragraphs push every decision far below the initial viewport.

At desktop size, verify all three opening responses fit in the initial incident panel. On mobile, scrolling within the panel is expected; verify equipment selection, each response, detailed evidence, pause, history and Menu remain reachable by touch. Expanded evidence must not cover the footer. Keep tap targets consistent with the existing buttons, visible focus, named controls, `aria-pressed` on selection/toggles, labelled charts and progress bars, and status announcements for inspection and pending actions. Do not rely on colour alone. Preserve reduced-motion styling. Tooltips open on hover (after a short pause) and on keyboard focus, close on Escape, and explain locked controls too. On touch a tap on a term opens its tooltip; tapping a button only acts.

## Review before changing UI

Compare the result with the current committed game's UI and this guide, not only with a standalone mockup. Existing visual references are in [the prototype migration notes](prototype-migration.md); source components and current browser renders are the maintained baseline.

Inspect title, healthy management, active incident, selected equipment, pending response, recovery report, history and Menu on desktop (1440 × 900) and a narrow touch viewport (about 390 px wide). Also check an unavailable action and long/large values. Confirm that warnings, control order, icon meanings and selected states remain familiar, exact evidence remains available, and actions have not gained or lost gameplay effects.

Check button dimensions and neighbouring control positions before and after interaction and asynchronous loading, including delayed account data, long player names and changing labels or counts. Width and height must remain stable through idle, loading, disabled, active, success and error states wherever those states apply.

Follow [testing.md](testing.md) for static checks and the real WebGL browser suite. Extend a browser journey for changed selection, disclosure or action behaviour; take screenshots for visual inspection. Passing tests establish wiring and reachability, not visual consistency by themselves. Include any intentional design departure and its gameplay reason in the PR description, and update this guide when that departure is approved.
