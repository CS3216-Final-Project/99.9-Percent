# UI design for 99.99%

This guide records the current playable game's design. It applies to both modes - presented to players as Guided Mode and Challenge Mode - including later phases. Gameplay rules may change; the shared visual language and interaction patterns should remain familiar. The proposal's future 2D direction is planning context and does not replace the current room or UI without a separate design decision. Gameplay rules live in [GAMEPLAY.md](GAMEPLAY.md), and the building, city and sound in [WORLD.md](WORLD.md).

## Chapter 0: Launch Week

Launch Week teaches the same ground rules by playing the real opening week, not by scripted lessons. Every real control stays enabled; a coach line names the next thing to do and a warm gold spotlight frames the control it means, dimming the rest. The clock runs freely from the first second; the company pauses itself only for real acknowledgements (a postmortem, the milestone, the closing score card). The opening letter is a real dialog with the company's launch metrics; it never blocks a control, and it closes on Open the doors.

Replay Launch Week is optional for every profile. It starts a fresh practice company on the same version-2 launch scenario; the paused original keeps its own save and is restored on Exit practice, including an active incident, and after reload or Save and exit. The overview labels the practice as in progress under the Launch Week entry. Profiles whose stored walkthrough record predates Launch Week count the introduction as done.

## Approved Warm Classic patterns

The whole playable game uses Warm Classic: title and account screens, both modes, Launch Week, later chapters, room controls, incidents, purchases, reports, Tech, Team, History, settings and feedback. Use cream paper surfaces, dark plum text and primary actions, warm gold accents, blue information, green success, lavender selection and restrained red/orange status throughout.

- **Flat controls:** 14 px rounded rectangles, thin warm borders, no bevels, hard offset shadows, press movement or inset shading. Only floating layers have soft diffuse shadows. Hover/press change fill and border; blue focus differs from lavender selection. Keep touch targets at least 44 px wherever practical and reserve stable button dimensions across states.
- **Icons:** shared Lucide icons in pastel concept tiles. Tech is gold and History blue, with matching tile geometry. Run/Pause has a plain icon without a yellow tile. Icon-only controls have accessible names; status uses text as well as colour.
- **Menu:** At a glance has a dashboard icon and separate Week, Game second, Users and Cash icon cards. Autosaved/Not saved is a quiet summary status. Matching compact Resume, Save now, How to play and Exit buttons share one row; Resume is primary and Exit uses logout. A failed save replaces the menu with a confirmation offering Cancel, Save and exit, or explicit Exit without saving. Preserve unreadable existing saves.
- **Clock:** calendar Week N, clock current second / 60 s and slim weekly progress. This is an operating week, not a maximum game duration. No separate Total seconds line. Preserve the authoritative elapsed counter for saves, tutorial logic and test instrumentation.
- **Workspace:** open by default; Hide equipment and actions collapses it to Equipment & actions. Reopening restores focus. Selecting equipment or entering an incident reveals it. It stays available during the mandatory guide and purchase quotes. No redundant System overview heading or divider.
- **Inspection:** timer, error and backlog symptom icons above Inspect equipment, without numbered steps. The Inspect equipment heading is plain text, without a tooltip or dotted underline. Selected equipment has its matching icon, name, Live tag and exact readings inline. Clicking the selected equipment chip again dismisses its readings; selecting another replaces them. Room inspections still select and reveal the equipment. No separate Full system evidence section or repeated measurement timestamp and utilisation explainer. Use the latest completed snapshot and preserve units; distinguish installed/routed capacity, measured/configured cache values, and actual/detected health.
- **Incident recovery:** keep chapter requirements above the Latency, Errors and Backlog cards during both healthy play and incidents. Below those readings, show Recovery progress with its game-second counter and always-visible conditions: latency below 500 ms, service errors below 1%, and accepted traffic with completed requests, for five consecutive game seconds. Explain that an unhealthy second resets progress and pausing freezes it. For an active failed server, also show that it must be restored or isolated from traffic with an empty queue. Recovery rules stay in the simulation.
- **Actions:** full name first; bold wallet cost and clock activation duration beneath it, left aligned. Purchases and incident responses share the structure while retaining their own engine rules and units. Keep unavailable reasons reachable and destructive changes reversible until confirmed.

Review all screens and every equipment selection at desktop and narrow mobile sizes, including keyboard focus, collapsed/open panels, loading/empty/error/success/disabled states, tooltips, long values and reduced motion. Preserve the scene, simulation, save compatibility and the Launch Week opening above.

## Visual language and reusable components

Use the shared font-size constants in `index.css`: `--text-xs` (0.8 rem), `--text-sm` (0.9 rem), `--text-md` (1 rem), `--text-lg` (1.15 rem), `--text-xl` (1.35 rem), `--text-2xl` (1.6 rem), `--text-3xl` (2.1 rem) and `--text-4xl` (2.9 rem). Body text starts at md; use sm and xs for secondary labels and larger sizes for headings and prominent values. Preserve browser zoom. Responsive display titles can interpolate between shared endpoints; ordinary text and controls use the scale rather than one-off sizes.

The isometric room is the main canvas. Warm Classic uses a dark plum HUD, cream paper panels, smooth Lucide icons and plum primary actions with pastel concept tiles. The agreed direction is warm and playful with flat controls: cream, plum and gold, colourful concept icons, a shared 14 px button radius, a 22 px panel radius and thin warm borders. Buttons, tabs, icon tiles, sliders and inputs never use bevels, inset shading, hard offset shadows or press-down movement. Soft diffuse shadows belong only to floating layers. Hover and press change colour and border without moving a control. The bottom dock uses the same dark purple shell as the top HUD in both modes. Its groups carry no visible headings, so the dock stays one control row tall; the clock uses light text; navigation controls stay cream, icon tiles stay pastel and selected view tabs stay lavender with plum labels. Tech uses a warm gold icon tile against its lavender selection so the icon never blends into the selected surface. The top HUD groups its four metrics on one cream surface with quiet internal dividers, pastel concept tiles, sentence-case labels and prominent plum values. Cash and App Health retain their actions, hover feedback and visible keyboard focus. Warning and failure values use the dark status inks against paper. Header Cash, Users and App Health keep their icons visible, including on phones. Use the tokens in `frontend/src/index.css` for colours, fonts, borders, radii, spacing and shadows. Pixelify Sans is the face for the 99.99% logo only, through `--font-logo`. Space Grotesk is the face for headings, controls, labels, metrics and room lettering; Source Sans 3 is the body face for sentences, longer explanations, tooltips and text entry. These are the only three game typefaces, bundled locally. Numbers use tabular figures. Keep canvas font loading and redraws in sync with the interface fonts so room signs, speech bubbles and billboards use the same typography. Do not introduce a separate theme or a compressed text-only HUD for a new mode.

Buttons must keep the same width and height across their states at a given viewport size. Account for every label, icon, badge and loading indicator before choosing dimensions: clicking, toggling, disabling, loading data or completing a request must not resize a control or move neighbouring controls. Reserve sufficient space from the first render with a shared size or a layout sized for the largest supported state; do not shrink the text to make a later state fit. Responsive sizes may change with the viewport, but state changes must not change the layout. For example, the home-screen Account chip must reserve its final footprint while account data loads and keep it when switching between Sign in and the player's identity; constrain long names within that space while keeping the full name accessible. Apply the same rule to Run/Pause, changing speed labels, counts and pending/success/error button labels.

The room itself is a realistic office, apart from the HUD's purple: warm off-white walls, concrete, grey carpet tiles where the crew work and coloured carpets in the rooms, charcoal and grey metal racks, white and light grey furniture, with a few colour accents. Its colours live in `frontend/src/components/scene/palette.ts` (`ROOM_PALETTE`, `FURNITURE`, `EQUIPMENT`, `PIXEL_FLOORS`); take new room colours from there rather than writing hex values into the scene. The HUD tokens in `index.css` are for the interface only.

The building has two floors on one footprint (`game/floors.ts`, `scene/levels.ts`): the office on top and the server floor below, where every machine stands; the people's desks (Deploy, Team, Growth) stay upstairs. The room shows one floor at a time, and only the floor the elevator stands at has labels and clickable equipment, so nothing on screen points at a floor that is not drawn. The floor menu opens from Floor (with a layers icon and selected floor number) in Navigation in the shared bottom dock, or from Navigation on phones, like an elevator's panel: one button per floor with its storey number, the floor in view pressed with the shared lavender selection, and a badge on a floor out of view that needs the player (amber for a warning, red and pulsing for an incident, with the reason in the button's accessible name). Switching rides the elevator in 5 seconds as a short cutscene: the view glides in to the shaft, tilting down to look along the glass car, and holds on it, letterboxed and softly vignetted, its hoist cables and counterweight moving in the shaft, dark for a moment as the car passes the slab between the storeys; light floods in as the doors part and the view eases back to where the player had it (or to framing the floor's machines again). It is hurried along by a second press, any click or a key, and cut straight through with reduced motion or when the player turns on Skip elevator cutscene in the menu's Graphics tab. A cut ride sounds only a single short bell, and a setting changed mid-ride applies from the next ride. Choosing equipment from a panel, the inspector or a tutorial step takes the view to its floor; an incident never moves it, but its panel offers Go to the server floor. Page Up and Page Down switch floors except while typing or in a dialog or menu. The floor in view is remembered in this browser, per mode, like the walk mode and camera below it; it is never part of a save.

Walk mode is optional and never the only way to do anything. View mode sits beside Floor and Camera under Navigation. It offers explicit Overview (pan, zoom and rotate), Walk mode (explore on foot), Follow (walk with the camera behind you) and First person (walk seeing through its eyes) choices, with the active choice pressed; V goes from one view to the next. Follow and First person turn walk mode on, and ending the walk returns to the overview centred on where the avatar was, facing the way the view did. On phones the same choices are inside Navigation. Selecting either closes its controls menu and hands the keys back to the room. The avatar wears a yellow ring so it stands out from the crew, and a yellow target marks a click-planned destination until it arrives. What it can reach is a button low in the middle of the room ("Space · Inspect Database"), so a tap works on a phone, with the same words in a polite live region for screen readers. Keys belong first to a text field, a dialog or a menu; the arrows also to a focused button, tab, radio or slider, while Space uses what the avatar can reach even then — only with nothing in reach does a focused control keep it. A fun thing the avatar plays with says so in the same button while its animation runs ("Space · Resting in a nap pod…"), and the avatar moves at a brisk walk with Shift to run. The crew notice the avatar walking up to them: a glance, a word in a speech bubble and the word said aloud in their own voice, once per approach and never in a chorus, though the sleeper and the elevator's riders keep to themselves. The crew's lines are spoken by the browser's own speech in a voice per species (pitch and rate by body plan), louder the nearer the avatar stands; a new line cuts the one before it off, and where the browser cannot speak a short bark in the body plan's register stands in. In the views from the avatar a chatting group's bubbles are only legible within earshot, a subtitle band above the walk prompt (under it on a phone) captions the line being said with the speaker's role named ahead of it while Show captions in the menu's Sound section is on, and right up close a member turns to greet the avatar. Esc closes in a fixed order: a dialog or controls menu, a view sheet, walk mode seen from above, then the selection. In a view from the avatar Esc only gives the cursor back; the Exit button leaves. Avatars are chosen from pictures in a radio grid, from a chip beside the account's on the home screen or the menu's Avatar tab; the choice is a browser setting, never part of a save.

The views from the avatar use a perspective camera beside the overview's orthographic one (`scene/views.ts`, `scene/ViewRig.tsx`). Moving between the overview and either of them dims to dark, cuts and comes back in 0.6 seconds, with a soft whoosh; between Follow and First person the camera eases. Reduced motion cuts at once. Riding the elevator from the avatar skips the overview's cutscene: the view simply rides with the car. A run opens in first person until the player chooses how to see the room (a view, or walk mode on or off), except that a guide about to point things out, or a `?floor=` address, opens from above; a guide that starts later takes over a walk the player never chose. Both views from the avatar take the mouse as they open: moving it looks around, Follow swinging round the avatar, and in first person a left click acts at the centre dot. The hint at the top draws the mouse as a key, moved or left-clicked, beside the words; it and the card centre on the room beside the equipment panel while the panel is open, wrapping rather than running under it. Escape gives the cursor back and a card in the middle offers Look around or Exit, and a touch screen drags instead, with Exit in its hint; Escape never leaves by itself. A dialog or a guide's card opening over the room gives the cursor back to reach it; an inspection's reading is the room answering a click, so looking around carries on and the next left click puts the reading away. Where the browser refuses the pointer there is no card: the view turns towards where the mouse is moved near an edge, and the hint has Exit. Follow stays inside the room, under the walls' tops and in front of the elevator shaft, so every wall stands. First person draws a ceiling of tiles, light panels and vents, a dot at the centre of the view, and a short hint at the top on how to look round, without keys on a touch screen. Equipment labels shrink with distance seen from the avatar and hide behind it. The city outside is not drawn there: the walls and ceiling hide it, and the windows show it as pictures. The menu's Controls tab holds the look speed, a switch turning looking up and down round, and the field of view, 70° to 90°.

The map is a round dial in the room's bottom left, clear of the equipment panel on the right and the walk prompt in the middle, and under the equipment sheet on a phone until the sheet is hidden. Like a game's minimap it is centred on the avatar while it walks the floor shown, otherwise on what the overview looks at, and turned so the way the view faces is up; it moves with the camera every frame, and an N on the rim points to the back wall. It is drawn as SVG from `scene/floorplan.ts`, so it cannot disagree with the room. It shows the floor in view with each zone tinted apart from the zones it meets, in five of the interface's tile colours, a fine line where they meet and the zone's name upright however the dial turns. Each zone's area is the one the location line names: the rooms behind glass, and otherwise the nearest zone's share of the open floor. Over the zones come glass, the shaft and furniture, the avatar as a yellow arrow, the wedge first person sees, the crew as dots and a red badge where an incident shows symptoms (blue while being investigated), waiting on the rim when it lies beyond. A chip underneath says where the avatar is, such as "Lounge", naming the floor only when it is not the one the map shows, and is the map's polite live region; the drawing is hidden from screen readers. Floor tabs across the top of the rim change floor like the floor menu, and in walk mode a click on the dial walks the avatar there. A zone glows for a moment as the avatar walks in. It starts folded to a Map button until the player opens it, and the close button folds it again; the choice is remembered in this browser.

The menu's Map tab is the same plan at pause-menu size, as a flat page rather than a dial — and drawn as a dark ops board: the HUD's night tokens, a faint one-metre grid, zones coloured by what they are for (machines, desks and meetings, breaks and fun, back of house), glass in the users' cyan and walls in paper white. Two floor tabs name each floor in full and badge the one out of view when it needs the player, and a line beside them says where the avatar is. A search box finds equipment, zones and things to do by name on both floors, and four pill toggles — dark when on and showing a check — choose what the plan shows: equipment, people, things to do and zone names. The plan drags to pan and scrolls to zoom about the cursor, framed by zoom buttons (in, out, fit the whole floor, and Centre on you, which switches floor if need be and zooms to at least twice fit), a five-metre scale bar and a hint; its labelled pins keep their size on screen however far the plan is zoomed, like a game's map blips, the avatar pulses where it stands, the elevator is labelled like a place, and a click picks a place or drops a destination marker. A legend under the plan names the four zone colours and every symbol — equipment, thing to do, person, you, incident — hiding the ones whose layer is off. The picked place's card says what and where it is, with its health and any incident badge, and offers "Show in room" (equipment selects it and centres the camera; a place on the other floor sends the room there) and "Walk here" while walk mode is on and the avatar stands on that floor — both hidden while a guide runs. A directory underneath lists the floor's equipment (each chip dotted with its state), places and things to do as counted groups of chips. Its accessible regions are named "Floor plan", "Map floor", "Show on the map", "Map zoom", "Selected place", "Places on this floor" and "Matches".

Each zone of the office has a floor of its own and its name floating over it (`ZONE_NAMES` in `scene/floorplan.ts`, `SERVER_ZONE_NAMES` downstairs): a small dark sign drawn in the 3D scene, always facing the camera and 20 px tall at any zoom, smaller than the equipment's labels and fading out when the camera pulls back too far to read it. Zone names are scenery, not interface: they carry no actions, ignore clicks and stay out of the accessibility tree. Machine rooms keep their equipment labels and get no zone name.

## Shared flat control system

Apply this system to both modes and every surface: title and account, HUD and chapters, overview and incidents, operations and work, footer and navigation, all nine menu tabs, purchase confirmations, inspection and reports, tutorials, Tech, Team, History, feedback and forms. The menu is not a separate theme. Night sheets retain their dark workspace; the controls and paper evidence cards use the same geometry and states. Tech, Team and History leave a 16 px outer gap inside the desktop room area and an 8 px gap around the mobile sheet. Clip the contents to the rounded panel, keep the title and Close action fixed above its scrolling body, and inset introductory text from the edges.

| State / role | Shared treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Primary decision | Plum fill, cream label and icon; darker fill while pressed. Use for Resume, Run, start and confirmation actions. |
| Secondary action | Cream fill and a thin warm edge; warmer fill on hover and press. |
| Selected / toggled | Lavender fill and plum edge, plus the existing selected, pressed or checked semantics. Radio choices retain a filled marker; status and selection never depend on colour alone. |
| Destructive action | Red treatment with an explicit action label and the existing confirmation. |
| Keyboard focus | Visible blue 3 px outline with an offset; do not replace it with a shadow or remove it on dark surfaces. |
| Disabled / unavailable | Readable muted text, no movement, and the existing explanation of the requirement or cost. |
| Loading / result | Reserve the control footprint across every label, count and icon change. |

Use `--control-edge`, `--control-inset`, `--control-hover`, `--control-active`, `--selection`, `--selection-edge`, `--primary-hover` and `--primary-active` from `index.css`. `--press` is `none`; `--lift` is a diffuse overlay shadow. Do not add a local palette to solve an individual component. Keep layout transforms, room animations, focus indicators and equipment-targeting rings independent from button decoration.

### Usability review

Use [Jakob Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) as review questions, not a usability score. A visual refresh must preserve these behaviours:

| Heuristic | Game review check |
| --- | --- |
| Visibility of system status | Time, saving, pending work and incident progress remain visible and accurately labelled. |
| Match with the real world | Player-facing equipment names and explicit cost/time units replace internal identifiers. |
| User control and freedom | Cancel, Escape, Resume and restored focus provide clear ways back. |
| Consistency and standards | The same action, state and icon have the same treatment across every game surface. |
| Error prevention | Purchase and unsaved-exit confirmations remain intact; unavailable choices explain why. |
| Recognition rather than recall | Actions keep their labels, costs, delays and contextual evidence. |
| Flexibility and efficiency | Existing shortcuts and full touch controls remain available. |
| Aesthetic and minimalist design | Show the next decision first; put supporting detail in existing disclosures. |
| Error recovery | Preserve plain errors, retry, the entered text and raw-save export. |
| Help and documentation | Keep contextual tooltips, How to play and explanations beside unfamiliar controls. |

## Screen structure

**Launch Week** replaces the scripted introduction with a playable one-week opening on the real simulation, on desktop and phones alike. A fresh Guided Mode company is a version-2 "opening-db" scenario whose one incident is a database overload: demand grows past the small database while the single app server keeps headroom, and the fix is the paid database upgrade. Use Launch Week as the introduction's name in the current chapter, the overview (entry 0), the home screen and related controls. Maya's Company Message shows once before the first Run; the week ends on a score card showing uptime in nines, the requests lost, a Copy result action and Continue, which awards the opening milestone and hands the company to Chapter 1. Each step is a spotlight card (in the coach strip above the dock on phones) that frames its real control and dims the rest: Run, the incident pause, inspect the Database, read its metrics, upgrade it, the recovery, End week, and the free research point's pick, which lands mid-week. The score card waits until the research is picked. A database upgraded before the overload lets the week complete without an incident. Every action is real, cash and upkeep are the launch scenario's own, and the clock only pauses for incidents and real acknowledgements. Upgrades not yet introduced stay locked. Three speed buttons and End week (20x, dropping back to normal when an incident opens) sit in the desktop dock; narrow docks and phones fold the speeds into the speed menu. The whole room stays furnished; Guided Mode keeps every machine's load bar up and a steady glow under the request path in every chapter (Challenge Mode does not show the load bars), and the stripped-down traffic view is the player's own choice. Chapters 1 and later keep the Hint button and their one-card guides; spotlight steps are only used in Launch Week.

- **Home screen:** one screen for every visit, whichever mode was played last: a first visit, a Guided Mode run in progress and a Challenge Mode run in progress look the same.
  The room fills the whole viewport behind the overlay, which darkens towards the card side.
  Feedback, avatar and account chips sit above the game name, which counts up from 0.00% to 99.99% on arrival (shown at once under reduced motion), then the tag line.
  The Guided Mode and Challenge Mode cards follow, Guided first, stacked on narrow screens; the section is named “Choose how you want to play” for assistive technology.
  Each card has a short explanation, a saved-game box and its own primary button; the phone edition adds a pace line.
  Guided Mode's explanation follows its save: begin with Launch Week, finish Launch Week, or work through the five chapters.
  The saved-game box reads “Launch Week in progress” while a company is in Launch Week (or, for a version-1 company, in the Opening before the retired walkthrough was done), “Chapter n of 5: name” after that (“of 6” for a version-1 company, which still plays the Opening), and “All chapters complete” or “Company closed” once the company has ended; Challenge Mode reads “Week n of 26”, “Last company reached the goal” or “Last company closed”; otherwise “No saved game”.
  The chapter comes from the company itself, so an imported or cloud save past the Opening shows its chapter even in a browser that never finished Launch Week.
  Guided Mode's button is Start Guided Mode, Continue company, or See how it ended for a company that has ended, which also offers New company.
  Challenge Mode's is Start Challenge Mode or Continue week n, and a run in progress adds New game.
  New company and New game replace a save, so the first press turns the button red as “Lose this company? Press again” or “Lose this run? Press again” with a warning, as in the menu.
  A completed Launch Week offers Replay Launch Week and a completed Challenge Mode guide offers Replay tutorial: Challenge Mode's restarts the first week, Guided Mode's starts a fresh practice company and restores the original company on Finish or Exit, and a company that has ended has none.
  Until Chapter 4, Stay Online, is complete, Challenge Mode is not a card: the Guided Mode card stands alone and a short teaser below it, with no controls, names the goal (“Grow to 50,000 users in 26 weeks on your own.”), says “Unlocks after Chapter 4: Stay Online.”, adds the player's current chapter once there is one, and shows one pip per chapter.
  The mode played last gets a yellow “Last played” tag, and on devices with a fine pointer keyboard focus starts on its button.
  Choosing the other mode switches to it at once and keeps the first mode's save; when the current run cannot be saved, the button turns red as “Switch and lose this run” with a warning inside that card, which points to Menu, Saves for export where the edition has it.
  No run starts before a choice.
  Anonymous analytics is on by default.
  Until the player answers, a one-line notice (“Anonymous gameplay stats help improve 99.99%.”) sits below the cards with a Details toggle that reveals what is tracked and that nothing is sent while the browser's Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control setting is on, plus equal OK and Opt out buttons; either choice is remembered and the notice does not return.
- **Account:** the chip reads Sign in (with “Save to the cloud”) for guests and shows the player's initials and name once signed in. It opens one Account dialog. Signed out, the dialog explains what an account is for, then shows Continue with Google, an “or use your email” divider and an email form that switches between Sign in and Create an account (optional name, password at least 8 characters with a show/hide toggle). Errors appear under the fields and keep what was typed. When accounts are offline, a warning callout with Try again replaces the status line and every option stays usable. Signed in, the dialog shows the profile with Sign out, then “This browser” (the Guided Mode save and Save to cloud, or why there is nothing to save), “In your account” (cloud saves with their time and version, each with Load, plus Refresh) and a Backup disclosure. Loading a cloud save closes the dialog and returns to the home screen with that save ready to continue. Say “cloud save” and “local save”, never “cloud company”.
- **Top bar:** the brand at the left, four familiar metric tiles, then the sound mute and Menu. The week or incident clock is not here: it sits in the bottom bar beside the buttons that advance it, so the player does not look across the screen between reading the time and moving it on. Health changes to the incident icon and the whole header gets the red striped treatment during an incident. Guided Mode pending revenue must be clearly distinguished from available cash and settled revenue.
- **Guided Mode chapters:** a compact bar below the company stats shows the current chapter number and name, one state-derived goal using `--text-lg` on desktop and phones (17.25 px at the game's default 15 px root size), Next when offered by the existing progression gates, and the existing View chapters button at the right. ## Guided Mode evidence and actions

Call physical simulation time **game seconds** throughout the interface: activation delays, evidence, recovery and chart axes. The total counter does not reset at week end. Every 60 game seconds settles an operating week: credit pending successful-request revenue, deduct accrued running costs and salaries, and record the result in History. Running continues unless another existing pause condition applies; non-positive cash after settlement closes the company. Weeks are compressed accounting periods, not real calendar weeks or chapter completion gates. Preserve internal field names, save formats and seeded timing.

Launch Week's guidance reuses the spotlight card shared by the chapter guides: a nonmodal card floating beside the playable game, the target in an undimmed window ringed in the warm gold spotlight colour (never the lavender selection colour), with a short label such as Click or Press Run pinned to it. The dim never takes clicks; a beat with nothing to point at shows the coach line alone, a clickable status line that advances to the real control when the player taps it. Place the card beside its target so it never covers it; below 900 px it docks to the top or bottom edge away from the target and keeps its reading compact. The coach line is dismissed by its own tap or by the action it points at; an outside tap advances to the next reveal or dismisses the final target. Only real game state drives a beat — a coach line never invents a button; an unresolvable target drops the overlay rather than pointing at nothing. The opening letter is a real modal (Launch week) with its own focus boundary; the score card (Launch Week complete) is a real modal too, with Copy result writing the nines line to the clipboard. Practice replays reuse the same company presentation: the chapter overview names practice in progress and offers Exit practice, and a reloaded or exited practice restores the original company including an active incident. Optional Challenge Mode and chapter spotlight guides share the card placement and keep their existing presentation.

Reuse the incident workspace, but use **Stable steps: n/5** and a stability meter instead of Challenge Mode's outage deadline. Explain the qualifying latency/error thresholds in the tooltip. Activation delays use physical steps. Inspection is free, immediate and repeatable; do not copy Challenge Mode's investigation timer or disable previously inspected equipment.

Show latency, service errors and total backlog as the compact symptom summary. Full system evidence must retain incoming/admitted/rejected demand; per-component demand, capacity, demand/capacity, busy utilisation, backlog, processed and failed counts; and installed versus routed server capacity. Cash and pending revenue stay in the top stats; the panel has no separate finances disclosure. Equipment readings lead with a short list of icon rows, the exact value on the right, toned green, amber or red; App Server and Database add demand against capacity as a load bar that is green, amber from 85% and red at capacity. Each App Server is a card with a routing status dot, its own load bar and its processed and backlog counts. Purchase previews show Setup, Cash after, Upkeep and Ready in as a 2 × 2 grid of tiles: red spending, green cash left (amber when the warning applies), amber upkeep and blue timing. Guided Mode actions lead with the shared icon for what they change. Inspect equipment starts directly with the component buttons: no request-path line and no separate pause button, since inspection is free and Run/Pause sits in the bottom bar.
Each campaign action shows what it changes in the room instead of in words: while the player points at or focuses an action, or its confirmation is open, the affected machine's marker and floor pad glow blue (Database for a database upgrade, Network for traffic limits and load balancing, Read Cache for cache work, Monitoring for Health Checks, the App Servers otherwise), and when that machine is on the other floor its floor button glows instead.
The panel lists each action only by name, price and activation; its effect and costs are not repeated there.
Pressing an action never buys it straight away. The view rides to the affected machine's floor and a confirmation opens over the room beside that machine's marker, with a notch pointing at it; a Scale up action points at its own App Server.
The camera eases to centre that marker in the part of the room the panel leaves open, at the default zoom whether the player had zoomed in or out; panning, zooming or dragging stops it.
The confirmation uses warm paper, a softly rounded outline and a plum primary button, matching other Warm Classic panels. Equipment targeting and the anchored notch retain their existing meaning.
The confirmation leads with the action and one short effect sentence, followed by the four quote tiles. Keep a routing requirement visible once when adding a server. How it works contains short labelled facts about the equipment and timing, plus any distinct action detail; show the forecast caveat only when a forecast exists. Do not repeat the delay, effect or routing note. Keep No refunds beside Confirm (focused) and Cancel in the pinned footer.
It follows the marker as the camera moves, sits on the marker's left, away from the panel, unless that runs off the room, when it takes the right, and when space is short the preview body scrolls while Confirm and Cancel stay pinned at its foot. Expanded details scroll into view and retain at least three readable lines on the supported short-phone viewport rather than collapsing beneath the warning.
Without a drawn room it settles in the open part of the stage.
Escape or Cancel closes it and returns focus to the action. Only one confirmation is open at a time, and Confirm follows the action's own disabled rule.

The UI reads the simulation snapshot and routes decisions through the store. It must not compute a second outcome, reveal a scripted root cause, recommend the matching fix, or restore weekly/timeout mechanics to make the layout look familiar. Costs, delays, unavailable actions and pending progress must reflect the existing rules. Use player-facing names such as “Database upgrade”, never internal IDs such as `upgrade-db`.

## Phone edition

Phones get their own edition, isolated from the desktop interface (`frontend/src/phone/`).
A device is a phone when its primary pointer is coarse and its screen is at most 500 CSS px on the short side; the choice is made once per page and never swaps the shell during a game.
Tablets, touch laptops and narrow desktop windows keep the desktop edition, and `?edition=phone` or `?edition=desktop` overrides the choice.
Phone styles are scoped under `[data-edition="phone"]` and the phone code loads only on phones, so the desktop interface never changes because of them.

The phone edition is gameplay only.
Guided Mode covers Chapter 0 and the first two chapters, Opening and Scaling & Routing, and then shows a phone campaign complete dialog while the company keeps running.
Finishing those chapters unlocks Challenge Mode, which plays in full.
The home screen holds the two mode cards and the analytics notice; there is no account, avatar, feedback, save files or playtest export.
The Menu holds the run card, Resume, the mode switch and only the Run, Sound and Graphics tabs, and the game autosaves silently.

The room gets most of the screen.
A 52 px top strip shows Cash and App Health with their icons, sound and Menu; Users and pending revenue sit behind one tap, with pending revenue still marked as not cash yet.
Guided Mode adds a one-line chapter row with the chapter, goal, Next and Hint, so the strip keeps its width for Cash and App Health.
System metrics is a bottom sheet over the room with peek, half and full states, changed by its handle button, a swipe or the arrow keys; selecting equipment opens it to half and an incident keeps it at least half open.
The canvas never resizes when the sheet moves.
Phones walk from above or behind the avatar: View mode offers Overview, Walk mode and Follow, the V key goes round those, and a remembered first person opens as Follow.
The room has no round minimap on a phone; the Menu's building map stays.
The dock is one 56 px row: views, Navigation, the clock, the speed menu, End week and the primary time action, with every target at least 44 px.

Launch Week's spotlight steps and the later chapter guides use a fixed coach strip directly above the dock, with the same box and button positions in every step.
It shows the progress, the title and the one-line task; Learn more opens the longer explanation and labelled examples in a non-modal reading layer that never covers the lesson's real control.
Each lesson sets the sheet state it needs, and the room keeps at least 40% of a 390 x 844 screen in every lesson.

## Responsive and accessible behaviour

Use the existing desktop side panel and mobile bottom panel. At 900 px and below, the header wraps and metric tiles retain their icons with compact values. App Health keeps Healthy on one line and wraps longer statuses within its card; its icon yields width before the text. At 360px and below, all four headline metrics share the compact `xs` text token. Purchase preview bodies scroll on short phones, with a readable expanded details area above the fixed warning and decision footer. At 640 px and below, the dock uses two rows: Time above, labeled view tabs and Navigation below. The speed selector remains available. The room stays usable above the mobile panel. Evidence tables may scroll inside their disclosure; the page must not overflow horizontally. Never show scrollbars anywhere in the game: `index.css` hides them globally (`scrollbar-width: none` and `::-webkit-scrollbar`) while wheel, touch, keyboard and trackpad scrolling keep working. Do not restore them with `scrollbar-width`, `scrollbar-color`, `scrollbar-gutter` or `::-webkit-scrollbar` styles on individual panels; indicate more content with layout, such as a clipped next row or a pinned footer. Do not make tables or explanatory paragraphs push every decision far below the initial viewport.

At desktop size, verify all three opening responses fit in the initial incident panel. On mobile, scrolling within the panel is expected; verify equipment selection, each response, detailed evidence, pause, history and Menu remain reachable by touch. Expanded evidence must not cover the footer. Keep tap targets consistent with the existing buttons, visible focus, named controls, `aria-pressed` on selection/toggles, labelled charts and progress bars, and status announcements for inspection and pending actions. Do not rely on colour alone. Preserve reduced-motion styling. Tooltips open on hover (after a short pause) and on keyboard focus, close on Escape, and explain locked controls too. On touch a tap on a term opens its tooltip; tapping a button only acts.

## Review before changing UI

Compare the result with the current committed game's UI and this guide, not only with a standalone mockup. Existing visual references are in [the prototype migration notes](prototype-migration.md); source components and current browser renders are the maintained baseline.

Inspect title, healthy management, active incident, selected equipment, pending response, recovery report, history and Menu on desktop (1440 × 900) and a narrow touch viewport (about 390 px wide). Also check an unavailable action and long/large values. Confirm that warnings, control order, icon meanings and selected states remain familiar, exact evidence remains available, and actions have not gained or lost gameplay effects.

Check button dimensions and neighbouring control positions before and after interaction and asynchronous loading, including delayed account data, long player names and changing labels or counts. Width and height must remain stable through idle, loading, disabled, active, success and error states wherever those states apply.

Follow [testing.md](testing.md) for static checks and the real WebGL browser suite. Extend a browser journey for changed selection, disclosure or action behaviour; take screenshots for visual inspection. Passing tests establish wiring and reachability, not visual consistency by themselves. Include any intentional design departure and its gameplay reason in the PR description, and update this guide when that departure is approved.
